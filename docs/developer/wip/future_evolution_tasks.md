## 1. Extension of the SystemVerilog Intermediate Representation (SV-IR)

The structural SystemVerilog Intermediate Representation (SV-IR) is currently restricted to validating and rendering the digital top-level wrappers (`crossbar_soc_top.sv` and `noc_soc_top.sv`). To establish complete structural validation across all generated wrappers, the SV-IR should be extended to cover the physical chip wrapper (`*_chip.sv`) and the universal Network-on-Chip node wrapper (`universal_tile.sv`).

### 1.1 Physical Top-Level Wrapper (`*_chip.sv`)
The physical wrapper instantiates the digital SoC core (`<project_name>.sv`), the padframe (`<project_name>_padframe.sv`), and the physical clock generators (e.g., PLLs, FLLs, crystal oscillators, and clock multiplexers).

#### Advantages
*   **Core-to-Padframe Interface Validation**: Enables static structural checking to verify that all digital ports exported by the SoC core map to the correct internal ports of the padframe (preventing pinout and naming mismatches).
*   **Clock and Reset Tree Validation**: Formally verifies the routing of clock generator outputs (e.g., PLL divided outputs) to the clock inputs of the digital core, and the connection of PLL status/lock signals to the system reset generators (`rstgen`).
*   **Parameter Verification for Clock IPs**: Inspects and validates parameter mappings (such as division ratios, lock counters, and bypass flags) against the clock generator cell models or technology catalogs.
*   **Unified Code Generation**: Replaces the legacy Mako-template string interpolation for `*_chip.sv` with the standard SV-IR rendering engine, improving code maintainability.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Temporal Build Dependency on Padrick**: The padframe RTL is generated by Padrick. The SV-IR cannot parse the padframe's ports until Padrick has written the files.
    *   *Mitigation*: The python orchestrator must ensure Padrick runs and writes the padframe sources before constructing the `*_chip.sv` IR. The `pyslang` parser can then compile the generated padframe wrapper signature dynamically.
*   **Handling of Mixed-Signal and Bidirectional Ports**: Padframes utilize bidirectional (`inout` tri-state) pins and analog lines (e.g., external loop filters, power rails, references). The current SV-IR data model is optimized for unidirectional digital signals (`logic`).
    *   *Mitigation*: Extend the `TopSignal` and `PortConnection` classes in `sv_ir.py` to support bidirectional signal types and analog bypass connections without triggering type-compatibility warnings.

---

### 1.2 Universal NoC Node Wrapper (`universal_tile.sv`)
The universal tile encapsulates the local NoC routing infrastructure, instantiating the router, local Network Adapters (NIs), and protocol conversion modules.

#### Advantages
*   **Router-to-NI Interface Verification**: Verifies that the internal links between the local router ports and the Network Adapter are wired correctly.
*   **Local Parameter Coherence**: Ensures critical NoC parameters (e.g., flit width, virtual channel count, and packet payload sizes) match between the router and adapter instances.
*   **Clock and Reset Domain Check**: Validates the instantiation of clock dividers, clock gating cells, and Clock Domain Crossing (CDC) synchronizers within the local node.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Engineering Overhead**: The internal structure of the `universal_tile.sv` is highly regular and static. Writing a dedicated Python builder for this module adds development complexity for a component that has a low probability of manual configuration errors.
    *   *Mitigation*: Keep the tile builder simple and standalone. Since the tile is rendered once as a parameterized module, the IR only needs to be constructed once per compile, avoiding grid-dependent boundary checking (which is already resolved and validated at the top-level digital wrapper).

## 2. Firmware Bring-Up of Gated Blocks

The System Controller defaults to `system_controller.power_on_state: "gated"`: every managed clock domain and every auto control group comes out of power-on clock-gated and held in reset. This is the safe hardware default and matches the gwaihir reference SoC.

The bring-up half of this chapter is delivered and lives in the user documentation: the generated testbench raises gated domains through architected agents (JTAG, serial link, UART - testbench guide, sections 3-4), the boot image travels an architected road on seven of the nine projects (`preload_mode`, testbench guide section 5; `crossbar_isle` and `noc_subtile` deliberately keep force+readmemh as the schema default's regression witness), and the firmware's own ungating is regression-tested by the offload application's phases and power cycles under `bring_up: minimal` (configuration guide 4.1-4.2, component standardization sections 8-9). What remains in this chapter: the nested boot (2.1), the offload residuals (2.2) and the negative-test suite (2.3).

### 2.1 A Generic Verification IP (`vip_ollivander_soc`)

The VIP and its agents (clock/reset, UART RX with the end-of-test detector, the JTAG stack, the serial-link twin, the uart-boot agent) are delivered and documented in the testbench guide (section 3); the boot and preload modes they serve are the configuration guide's sections 4.1-4.2. The single residual of this entry is the nested boot below.

#### What remains: the nested boot (the macro export ladder's last rung)

1. **Nested boot, then the final `super_noc` measure.** The full dividend — the nested Cheshire instances leaving a super's top — requires the parent to bring up the nested macro hosts architecturally instead of by force.

    **Design settled on 2026-08-17 (user proposal, verified in-tree): the parent's FIRMWARE is the agent, over AXI through the macro window - not a second JTAG TAP.** No macro JTAG export, no daisy chain, no VIP work: this is the ownership split section 5.4 already states (the testbench acts once before the core exists; everything after the boot handoff belongs to the running program), and it makes a nested SoC just another offload target - one whose wake sequence writes a Cheshire's scratch registers instead of a boot-address register and a wake wire.

    **The fact that makes it cheap**: the macro top TRANSLATES addresses at its boundary (`axi_req_i.aw.addr - MACRO_BASE_ADDR`, `noc_soc_top.sv.mako`), so inside the macro the map is exactly the standalone project's map. Two consequences that remove the rungs we feared: the child's own generated firmware is **reusable verbatim** - it is linked for the map the child still sees, so no relocation and no regeneration against the parent's placement - and the window contract collapses to one arithmetic identity, `parent_address = window_base + child_internal_offset`, where the parent already owns the base (it assigned it) and the child already publishes its offsets in its generated headers (`<child>_map.h`, `<child>_regs.h`) and IP-XACT. The remaining work is exposing and including them, not designing them. A sizing note from 2026-08-22, settled the same day: the ELF loader's STATIC section buffer (Verilator cannot take dynamic arrays at DPI open arrays) is a per-project knob, testbench.elf_max_section_bytes, default 4 MiB - a derivation from destination windows was rejected precisely because a nested target only shows the parent the macro's aggregate window. A nested-boot image larger than the default therefore needs nothing from this ladder: the project raises the knob. Coherence constraints of the firmware-reuse hypothesis (analyzed 2026-08-22, user question): (1) the child's FW and the macro RTL must come from the SAME donor generation - the (RTL, firmware) pair is one artifact, addresses and register offsets are baked in; (2) the parent must keep the macro window INTACT - aligned base, nothing of the parent's decoded inside the child's span, or child-FW accesses get hijacked; (3) load destinations come from the child's published map, same-generation as (1); (4) handoff writes take DESTINATIONS in parent view but VALUES in child view - with 'image: elf' the entry arrives child-view for free from get_entry(), where a parent-map literal would be wrong by construction, and the child's scratch registers are reached through its external AXI ingress, the path the SBA anomaly does not touch; (5) the genuinely open point is the COMPLETION CONTRACT: the child FW signals EOT on its own UART, unobserved in the parent's bench - export-and-monitor or a parent-visible mailbox, to be designed with the boot itself.

    **Decomposition** (point (a) reworked on 2026-08-18, when the self-preload mechanism was rolled back - see section 5.3.2): (a) the parent LOADS the child's boot image through the window - its firmware (or the JTAG agent, pre-handoff) writes `<child>/generated/sw/<app>.hex` word by word at `window_base + boot_memory_offset`, exactly the loader role silicon requires; slower in simulation than a `$readmemh`, but it needs no path into the macro and doubles as a test of the window's write path. A Questa-only accelerator (dotted `$readmemh` into the macro from the parent testbench) remains possible as an optimization, but the architectural loader is the portable mechanism; (b) the parent's firmware ungates the child's domains by writing the child's system controller through the window - what it already does for its own groups, plus an offset; (c) the boot handoff writes the nested Cheshire's scratch registers (entry, argc, go) the same way, which is the JTAG agent's sequence expressed as stores; (d) **observability is the one genuinely open point**: the child's UART is not exported by the macro, so its greeting never reaches the transcript. Either export the UART in the macro contract, or have the parent read a completion location of the child - the latter needs no new pins and fits the existing `Offload*` contract.

    Queue position (2026-09-02, user decision): after the CSR-programmable stamper and the simulation progress monitors. One motivation added the same day: the architectural loader is also the only nested boot compatible with the Verilator hierarchical flow, since it never reaches into a `hier_block` - the dotted `$readmemh` accelerator would.

    Priority note (2026-08-17, discharged 2026-08-23 by the slink-only wave): the parent's own boot mode was the bigger blocker - `super_noc` was force-mode, keeping its own manager tile and preload memories inlined - and has since migrated to `slink`. The `super_noc` build re-measure against the PCH baseline happens once, after this task, with everything in place.

### 2.2 Cluster Offload Tests: Open Residuals

The `offload` application is the standard firmware of every example project and passes the full `TEST_SIM=1` suite under QuestaSim on both contract kinds, arrays included; the behaviour is user documentation (`docs/soc_configuration_guide.md` section 5.1, `docs/hw/component_standardization.md` section 9, `docs/hw/component_standardization.md` section 2.6). Only the open residuals live here:

*   **Global hart IDs overlap across mesh cluster arrays.** Every `cluster_subtile` instance receives `hart_base_id_i = '0`, so all 16 clusters carry harts 0..8 and `mhartid` identifies a core only within its own cluster — which is why the offload payload addresses everything through the alias region. Unique global IDs would take a per-instance hart base (the wiring point exists now, next to the per-instance window base) plus a payload demux change (`mhartid % NumCores` instead of `mhartid - hart_base`); decide whether debug/trace infrastructure ever needs the uniqueness before spending the change.
*   **Payload icache enablement.** The payload runs with the cluster icache in bypass: every fetch reaches L2 (thousands of refill requests for a two-line park loop). Functional, but slow and unrepresentative — pulp-runtime enables the icache first thing. Needs the icache-ctrl offsets in the contract, so the payload prologue can turn it on.
*   **Multi-core payloads.** Core 0 works, cores 1..N park in `wfi`; rv32im has no atomics, so a parallel payload leans on the TCDM test-and-set alias or per-core result slots. Deliberately out of phase 1.

### 2.3 Negative Tests: Proving the Suite Detects Failure

First live specimen (2026-08-12): the run-leg pass criterion accepted any run with UART output, and a Verilator run that died after the greeting was recorded SUCCESS for 3h09 of grinding - exactly the class of silent false positive this chapter exists to make impossible. The criterion now requires the EOT marker too, but only a negative-test suite proves there is no second hole.

The suite has no expected-failure test: the offload application's failure policy (report on the UART, park WITHOUT the EOT so the testbench timeout turns the run into a FAILED) is designed but never exercised by the regression - if that path silently broke, a failing firmware would pass the suite and nobody would know. Gwaihir treats this as a first-class test class (`sanity_fail` returning an exact nonzero code, CI matching `FAILED: return code 7` literally and requiring exactly one error line; a cluster-side variant proves the per-core return path with an exact aggregate). Recorded 2026-08-10 from the reference analysis.

*   **Shape for Ollivander**: a generated expected-failure variant (a `test_app` name or a suite knob) that makes the firmware return a known nonzero code or corrupts the offload expectation on purpose, plus a suite leg asserting the run is reported FAILED for that exact reason. Small, near-term, independent of every other task.
*   **Bonus from the same source**: the memory_mapped host check can adopt gwaihir's sum-of-return-codes convention, which makes multi-core negative expectations exact ((cores) x (code), e.g. 64 x 14 = 896 in their CI).

#### Advantages
*   **Protects the failure path itself**: the one part of the test machinery that no passing regression can ever validate.
*   **Nearly free**: the firmware and criteria already exist; only the expected outcome flips.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **The suite equates FAILED with regression**: an expected failure must not turn the summary red.
    *   *Mitigation*: the suite leg inverts the criterion for the negative project/app (expects the FAILED marker and the exact diagnostic), keeping the summary green when the failure is the designed one.

## 3. Open Decisions from the Simulation-Log Reviews

Everything here is visible in the logs by design: the reviews narrowed the message-suppression list in `src/templates/sim.mk.mako` (`2732` downgraded instead of suppressed, `3999` and `8602` gone, `2912` and `2241` no longer suppressed by the fast-check `vopt`), so every open item below has its warning signature. One recurring family is deliberately *not* listed: the `vopt-2685`/`vopt-2718` (TFMPC) dangling-port warnings were audited across all seven projects and are upstream instantiations leaving optional ports unconnected — unread outputs, or inputs consumed only under configuration bits the instantiating clusters set to zero — with nothing to fix on our side. None of the items below is a mechanical fix: each changes design behaviour, or asks what to do about a defect in an IP we do not own.

### 3.1 The HWPE Path: hci Remapping and redmule Widths

Upstream `hci` from v2.6.x provides multi-precision support (per-tile bank-word geometry), but adopting it breaks the crossbar family: `cluster_peripherals` drives `hci_ctrl_o.low_prio_max_stall`, a single 8-bit "maximum consecutive stalls" field that upstream replaced with an `arb_policy`, `priority_cnt_numerator` and `priority_cnt_denominator` triple. No branch or release of that IP, in the CHIPS-IT fork or upstream, speaks the new struct, so the catalogue pins the CHIPS-IT `hci` fork for what it does *not* have. The remapping is work on two repositories CHIPS-IT owns and Ollivander only consumes: one line in `cluster_control_unit.sv` of the `cluster_peripherals` fork, and then the `rev` that `pulp_cluster` pins it at — the catalogue does not declare `cluster_peripherals`, it arrives transitively. Both changes go through CHIPS-IT's own PR process, so they are recorded as the two enabling PRs in `upstream_pr_candidates.md` (CHIPS-IT forks section).

In the same logs, two connections inside `redmule` mismatch the width of the port they drive: `redmule_w_buffer` reaches `redmule_w_buffer_scm.wdata_i` with 480 bits against a 512-bit port, and `redmule_x_buffer` reaches `redmule_x_pad_scm.wdata_i` with 480 against 448 — the second truncates 32 bits. All three figures are multiples of the 16-bit format, a discrepancy of two elements in each direction. Ollivander overrides only `ID_WIDTH`, `N_CORES`, `DW` and `HCI_SIZE_tcdm` on `redmule_top`, leaving the array geometry (`ARRAY_HEIGHT`, `PIPE_REGS`, `TOT_DEPTH = DATAW/BITW`) at the IP's own defaults, so whether this is an internal inconsistency of those defaults or an interaction with the data width we impose is not established.

*Decision to take*: whether to do the remapping now or when multi-precision is actually wanted.

#### Advantages
*   **Unblocks upstream `hci`**, which would retire a CHIPS-IT fork whose two fixes are meanwhile upstream verbatim, and would put that entry on a release rather than an untagged commit. The registry audit of 2026-08-29 raised the stakes: the move also retires three registry patches — the `hci_router` `r_evalid` repair (fixed upstream by the multi-precision refactor) and both `datamover` dead-port patches (they live and die with the forcing) — and picks up two genuine `r_ready` fixes in `hci_core_split` (`ad31d27`, `142769d`) that the fork lacks. The `hci_core_split` `r_evalid` self-assignment is still live upstream, so that one patch is re-expressed against the new tree rather than dropped.
*   **Makes multi-precision reachable**: a tile could then request a bank word other than 32 bits and have the request honoured instead of discarded.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Two open questions, one trigger.** The remapping and the `redmule` width mismatches both become relevant the moment the HWPE accelerator is exercised.
    *   *Mitigation*: resolve both when a test drives the accelerator, so the answers can be verified instead of derived. Until then, the interim step — dropping the `low_prio_max_stall` assignment and accepting a firmware-visible register with no effect — is available but should not be taken just to quieten a log.
*   **The new arbitration semantics are not a rename.** A maximum stall count and a numerator over a denominator are different controls, so the mapping has to be derived from the arbiter, and the register bits the firmware writes have to keep meaning something.

### 3.2 CAN Timestamp, and the Tie-Offs Inherited from astral

`src/templates/hw/isles/apb_subsystem_isle.sv.mako` ties the `timestamp` input of `can_top_apb` to `64'hFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`. The CTU CAN FD uses that port as its timebase, so a frozen maximal value makes every time-triggered transmission look already expired and stamps every received frame identically. The tie-off is inherited from astral, together with the others of the subsystem (the full inventory, with provenance, is the PROVENANCE note at the head of the template); they belong to one decision, not four:

*   `can_top_apb.timestamp` tied to all-ones (no timebase).
*   `apb_adv_timer.ext_sig_i` tied to `'0`: the 64 external trigger inputs are unusable. Astral marks it `/* TODO connect me */`.
*   `aon_timer`: `alert_rx_i`, `lc_escalate_en_i` and `sleep_mode_i` tied to `'0`, `alert_tx_o` left open, so the OpenTitan alert and sleep interfaces are inert.
*   The CAN and watchdog interrupts do not pass through an edge propagator, unlike the two timers. Astral makes the same distinction, so this is parity rather than an omission.

*Decision to take*: what to drive the CAN timestamp with — the real-time clock counter, the CLINT `mtime`, or a dedicated 64-bit counter inside the APB subsystem — and whether the remaining tie-offs are worth connecting at all, given that no example currently uses the interfaces behind them.

### 3.3 Assertions in the Regression

`ASSERTIONS` defaults to `1`, so a manual `make run-sim` does evaluate SVA. The test suite, however, forces `ASSERTIONS=0` at `soc_cfg_examples/ollivander_test.mk:204`, adding `-nosva -noimmedassert -nopsl`: **`make test-all` never checks a single assertion**, on any project — the FlooNoC `SupportLoopback` assertion, for instance, can only be verified by hand.

Related, and worth raising upstream regardless: with collectives enabled FlooNoC assigns the `collect_op_e` enum from a raw 4-bit vector without a cast, roughly 250 times per run (`floo_reduction_unit`, `floo_reduction_arbiter`, `floo_output_arbiter`, `floo_router`, `floo_nw_chimney`). Nothing therefore checks that the collective opcode carried in a flit header is a legal enum value. Message `8386` is suppressed purely for volume.

First catalogued specimen for that decision (2026-08-15): cluster_icache's `multi_accept_rr_arb` fires "It is disallowed to deassert the selected unserved request signals" at 2.3 us inside spatz on `crux_isle`, only under `ASSERTIONS=1`, i.e. outside the suite's conditions; the run is otherwise healthy through the full offload, and the firing predates the de-typing work (verified against a pre-transform tree). Whether the retracted icache request is legitimate boot-time behaviour or a real integration defect with no functional symptom today is exactly what the decision below has to weigh.

Second and third specimens (2026-08-21, on `crux` under jtag boot, well characterized because they were initially mistaken for a regression of the JTAG preload work): the same `multi_accept_rr_arb` assertion fires at 68.730 us - twenty nanoseconds after spatz's software reset release, while `l2` is still in reset - as an *immediate* `$fatal`, together with opentitan kmac's `RunThenComplete_M` in the security island (its window opens 1.1 us earlier, at that island's own release). Both are deterministic to the picosecond, insensitive to `-voptargs=+acc`, and independent of the testbench's observer blocks and of the preload mode (an A/B with stimulus-identical testbenches fails identically). The methodological trap that made them expensive: a pilot validated with a bare `make run-sim` runs a STRICTER criterion than the suite (`ASSERTIONS=1` against the suite's forced `ASSERTIONS=0`), so a project can be suite-green and manual-red without any code having changed - the triage below should also decide which of the two is the reference criterion for pilots.

*Decision to take*: whether the suite should run with assertions enabled — everywhere, or on a designated subset of projects.

#### Advantages
*   **Catches what warnings cannot**: protocol violations show up where they happen, not as a wrong result thousands of cycles later.
*   **Covers the NoC configuration space**: the collective and multicast paths we enable are guarded by upstream assertions that the regression currently discards.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Runtime and noise**: assertions slow the run down, and third-party IPs are known to fire benign ones during reset.
    *   *Mitigation*: enable them on one crossbar and one NoC project first, triage what fires, and only then extend. Keeping `ASSERTIONS=0` available per project means a noisy IP never blocks the whole suite.

### 3.6.1 Collectives: Generated, Enabled, and Never Exercised (2026-08-26)

A correction to a common assumption first: Ollivander DOES support FlooNoC's collectives, and the whole NoC family ships with them on. The emitted configuration enables four of the five switches floogen offers, and the per-component `en_collective` follows a feature the SoC description can declare - `mesh.yml` gives `compute_clusters` a `features: { multicast_target: true }`, so all sixteen clusters are multicast targets, with the mask sized on the address width.

What nothing does is USE them. The offload firmware writes to the clusters one by one, and touches neither barrier nor reduction. So the state is "hardware present, exercise zero" - the same shape of exposure as the chip wrapper before the ninth wave: generated logic that no flow puts under load. Four features are enabled and none is verified; if the masks, the reduction pipeline depth or `cut_offload_intf` were wrong in our configuration, nothing would say so.

**The fifth switch, `en_narrow_reduction`, is off - and that is inherited, not decided.** gwaihir's three configurations carry the identical block, comment included (`# selective FP ops, default hw config`), so our omission came with the template. What we give up is the integer ALU reduction on the narrow router (`EnA_{Add,Mul,MinS,MinU,MaxS,MaxU}`) - which is arguably the more useful set for collecting control results: a count, a maximum index, a sum of status flags. Our offload test checks outcomes by reading each cluster's memory, exactly the job an integer reduction would do in one transaction.

**And leaving it off appears to avoid a real problem** (colleague's report on the MAGIA project, 2026-08-26). With FlooNoC 0.8.4 and floogen aligned, enabling narrow reduction breaks the simulation under QuestaSim - reproduced on 2025.2.1, 2025.3 and 2026.3, with `-O5 -permissive`. Three details make the report diagnostically valuable:

*   **`+acc` makes it work again**, while lowering `-O` does not. Those are different levers: `-O<n>` governs machine-code optimisation ("disables few machine code optimizations", per vopt's own help), whereas `+acc` governs OBJECT VISIBILITY - whether vopt may remove, merge or collapse design objects. So the transformation that breaks it is structural elaboration, not code generation, which is exactly why `-O0` does not help.
*   **Three Questa versions, same behaviour**: not a release regression, but a systematic interaction - which points at RTL relying on something the tool is entitled to optimise away rather than at a tool defect.
*   **Only the narrow path fails**, while wide reduction works.

**A hypothesis, and its refutation** (2026-08-26). The guess was that the trigger is not narrow reduction but narrow reduction WITHOUT pipeline or cut, since floogen's model defaults `rd_pipeline_depth` to 0 and `cut_offload_intf` to False while our wide channel overrides both. The colleague's actual configuration refutes it: she writes `NarrRedCfg: RedDefaultCfg` by hand in the RTL, and `RedDefaultCfg` in `floo_pkg.sv:329` is `'{RdPipelineDepth: 5, CutOffloadIntf: 1'b1}` - the very configuration we give the wide channel. So her narrow reduction is already configured well, and the workaround built on that guess is void.

The refutation LOCALISES the defect instead, which is more useful: the difference between her setup and ours is only the CHANNEL. Her `OpCfg` has the six integer ops on and the four FP ops off; ours is the mirror image - and both use the same reduction hardware configuration. So what breaks is the integer reduction path itself, not how it is configured. That is a controlled comparison, and it is what an upstream report should lead with: same `RedDefaultCfg`, integer channel broken under vopt's object removal, FP channel healthy. The remaining mitigation is the scoped one - `-access=<spec>+<selection>` on that module alone rather than global `+acc`, whose help text warns of "a large negative impact".

**A separate defect found on the way, and it is upstream's.** FlooGen and FlooNoC disagree on what "default" means for a reduction channel:

| | `RdPipelineDepth` | `CutOffloadIntf` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RedDefaultCfg` in the RTL (`floo_pkg.sv:329`) | 5 | 1 |
| the floogen model's default (`routing.py:95`) | 0 | false |

So `en_narrow_reduction: true` in a floogen configuration emits depth 0 and no cut, while writing `RedDefaultCfg` by hand in RTL gives depth 5 and a cut. Two paths both called the default, producing different hardware, with the generator's being the worse of the two. It is the same generator-versus-library drift as chapter 3.7, found a third time and this time not in our code - worth a second, independent upstream report, which is also easier to get accepted than a simulator-interaction bug.

**Superseded hypothesis, kept for the record.** `NarrowReductionCfg` and `WideReductionCfg` derive from the same base with the same defaults - `rd_pipeline_depth: 0`, `cut_offload_intf: False`. The configuration we and gwaihir use OVERRIDES both for the wide channel (depth 5, cut true) and, writing `en_narrow_reduction: true`, would take the defaults for the narrow one: a combinational path where the wide has five pipeline stages and a cut. So the trigger may not be "narrow reduction" but "narrow reduction without pipeline or cut", and the first experiment is to configure it like the wide one. If that settles it, the workaround and the upstream report are the same sentence.

The other experiment is cheaper on performance than global `+acc`, whose help text warns of "a large negative impact": vopt accepts `-access=<spec>+<selection>[+<recurse_level>|.]`, so visibility can be granted to the narrow reduction module alone and the rest of the design left fully optimised.

Her tree is elsewhere.

**The phase order is now a stated rule, and it came from a failure (2026-09-01).** Two collective streams must never be in flight toward the same node: the reduction join admits only same-stream flits and the ones it waits for can queue behind the ones it ignores. So each phase must be DRAINED before the next, and the drain must be observable by every participant, not just the issuer - which ranks them: barrier first (it drains itself: a member's store returns only when the whole group has written), reduction next (internally serialized, closed by a read of the final sum), multicast last (its drain is the least observable, and nothing follows it). The barrier-only profile wedged with the multicast placed immediately before the barrier - the issuer's response collection and the barrier's merge converging on the same node - while `noc` had been passing on the old order by accident, its reduction phases acting as an unintended separator.

*B0 - turn the DCA on, alone.* `enable_dca: true` in the snitch-cluster `cfg/default.json` patch (`ollivander_config.yml`, beside `cluster_base_expose`) plus the wrapper regeneration the registry already runs. *B1 - a wide burst, with no collective at all.* The Snitch cores store 64 bits; the only wide master is the cluster iDMA, driven by CUSTOM INSTRUCTIONS (opcode `0x2b`: `dmsrc`/`dmdst`/`dmcpyi`/`dmstati`) executable only by the LAST hart (index `NrCores-1` = 8, the DM core - on any other hart they trap as illegal). No memory-mapped control path exists. Our toolchain needs no new `-march`: the mnemonics live only in the Snitch LLVM, but `.insn r 0x2b, 0, <funct7>, rd, rs1, rs2` assembles under plain `rv32im_zicsr/ilp32` (verified). Sequence: `dmsrc lo,hi` / `dmdst lo,hi` / `dmcpyi txid,size,(ch<<2)|0` / poll `dmstati busy,(ch<<2)|2`. Source is TCDM through the cluster alias, size a MULTIPLE OF 64 B so iDMA emits full 512-bit beats instead of strobed narrow ones. Stop condition: the transfer shows up in `dma_trace_00008_00000.log` (today 0 bytes - no transfer has ever been issued) and on the `floo_wide_*` channels. This milestone validates the DMA programming with no collective machinery in the picture.

*B2 - the wide injection path.* Structural, not configuration: the tile passes the collective types only on the narrow side, so the chimney's wide user is 1 bit wide and every wide injection is Unicast. *B3 - contract and guards.* A wide collect slot, with a constraint the narrow slots do not have: the wide beat is 64 BYTES, so the alignment guard must use the WIDTH OF THE WINDOW'S CHANNEL instead of the narrow beat it hardcodes today. *Measured against the references (2026-09-01), before writing a line.* **gwaihir has the wide reduction working**, on the same IPs, and settles what the plan above could only infer. The 512-bit beat is NOT a single operand: `dca_fork` splits it into **eight FP64 lanes**, one per compute core's FPU, in SIMD, and recomposes the result by concatenation (`vectorial_op` stays 0 - the vectorization is in the fork, not in the FPU). Their software verification confirms it from the other side: expected values computed per element with `atol=0`. Their cluster is generated with `enable_dca`, `enable_narrow_collectives` and `enable_wide_collectives` all true and a 52-bit wide user (`{collective_mask: 48, collective_op: 4}`); their test emits only from the DM core, allocates the buffers at the SAME TCDM offset in every cluster (a hard requirement), and puts a barrier between the row and the column phase because only ONE reduction may be outstanding per router - independently arriving at the phase-order rule wave A derived from a failure. Two structural constraints they assert and we must respect: `NrCores == 9` (eight compute plus the DMA core, so `dca_fork` has its eight lanes) and `DcaDataWidth == WideDataWidth == 512`. Their experience is the negative confirmation of the element-wise answer.

*B2bis - the injection path: DECIDED 2026-09-01, `dmuser` for the wide.* Gwaihir does NOT use a stamper on the wide: the DM core writes the AW user directly through the custom `dmuser` instruction before starting the transfer, which is the upstream-intended route and needs no second stamper - but it requires regenerating the cluster with `enable_wide_collectives` so that its `user_dma_t` grows from one bit to `{mask, op}`, which in turn changes the wide user width the subtile declares and the checks that keep that literal honest. Our own route extends the stamper to the wide path, leaves the cluster untouched and keeps the widened user confined between stamper and chimney, at the cost of a second instance and of a window alignment that must follow the 64-byte wide beat rather than the 8-byte narrow one. The decision is `dmuser`, on the merits rather than on what we already own. On the wide channel the only master IS the DMA, so the "DMA traffic only" limitation costs nothing, and in exchange: op and mask become RUNTIME values instead of elaboration constants; no alias region is needed, so the address says where and the user says how, instead of the address carrying both; the transfer size stops being a hardware constant (a stamper window must span the whole burst, which is tolerable for a 64-byte test and not for the 4 KiB transfers a real workload issues); nothing is added to the datapath; and the result speaks the API the Snitch runtime already offers. What it costs, and must be budgeted: regenerating the cluster with `enable_wide_collectives`, the wide user width the subtile declares growing from one bit to 52 with the elaboration checks that keep that literal honest, and the area of carrying those bits through the cluster's internal wide crossbar - which the stamper route would have kept outside. The stamper stays the only possible route on the NARROW, where no CPU store can drive per-transaction user bits: MAGIA reached the same design independently, which is the strongest confirmation available that there is no third way.

*Out of this wave's perimeter, recorded 2026-09-03: a MIXED group, one collective across the heterogeneous tiles.* The network would not object - a group is a mask over tile ids, and PULP, Spatz and Snitch all produce the same 32-bit stores, barrier bits and multicast writes - but the generator's group is "the instances of one component", and three assumptions follow from it: one power-of-two block of addresses indexed by the tile id (every destination sees the address inside its own window, which is why the boxes must be aligned and column-fastest), the same slot offsets in every member (the offset inside the tile is one for all destinations), and meta, expectations and stamper windows derived per component. *Functionally, in a generated SoC, heterogeneous collectives make sense for two of the three operations (assessment of 2026-09-03).* MULTICAST is the natural case: the same weights, descriptors, "go" words or payload images to consumers of different kinds, or replicas of a buffer into several memory tiles - the only requirement is that the destination offset means the same thing in every tile. THE RULE FOR THE WAVE, therefore: every crossing half on the POWER-ON reset of its own domain (`pwr_on_rsts_n[DomainIdx_<dom>]` on the domain side, `host_pwr_on_rst_n` on the network side - the reset tree already asserts them together and releases each synchronously, rstgen), never on the group or software reset; the isolation drain the firmware performs before a warm reset keeps the FIFOs empty across it, as today. No probe needed; a static fact. What remains to build: the group reset bit - a flop in the host's domain - must have its RELEASE synchronised into the tile's domain clock (a two-flop synchroniser in the tile), which today is unnecessary because the clocks coincide. (2) THE WIDE OFFLOAD INTERFACE HAS NO CROSSING: the router hands operands to the isle's DCA on the network clock; this wave refuses `wide_reduction` on a component outside the host's domain, with the reason, and leaves that crossing to a later wave (`noc_isle` reduces on the narrow channel, in the tile's ALU, and is not affected). (3) SIMULATION TIME: a half-speed domain doubles the payload's and the collectives' cycles (`noc_isle` from 11 to perhaps 15-18 minutes per simulator) and every tile changes ports, so Verilator rebuilds them all. (4) WHAT THE SIMULATION CANNOT SHOW: a divided clock is in phase with the network clock, so the witnesses prove that data and order cross correctly, not that the cells withstand truly asynchronous clocks - that guarantee is the cells' own and their synthesis constraints, as for everything the crossbar family already crosses; to be stated in the docs as a limit of the instrument. (5) `super_crossbar` INHERITS THE DOMAINS by nesting `mesh_isle`: a mesh macro with internal domains has never been compiled in a parent (the favourable precedent is `crux_isle`, six domains, inside `super_noc`) - generate and simulate it early in the wave, not only at the gate.

*The original entry, kept for the parts still open:* During the long stretches of an offload run only `[TB_TIME]` prints, and a wedge looks exactly like progress: the wide-write stall of 2026-09-02 (fifteen instances with a transaction that never completed) stayed invisible for hours, when a per-tile activity vector would have shown it at the first heartbeat. Three levels, by value over cost: (1) the ENRICHED HEARTBEAT - activity counters per tile and channel, reset at every beat, printed next to `[TB_TIME]` as a compact vector, so "working" and "stuck" read differently at a glance; (2) a PROGRESS MAILBOX - a phase word the firmware writes (the host for its phases; the payloads already have `meta`/`GO`/`DONE`) and the testbench watches and prints with the time, because UART is the wrong instrument: a forty-character line costs ~350 us of simulated time at 115200 baud and sits on the host's critical path, which is why the existing prints are sparse; (3) collective-specific monitors (stamper injections, DCA offloads, landings) behind a verbosity switch, valuable in debug and noise in regression. Ground rules: simulation-only (`ifndef SYNTHESIS`), never through UART, default silent (a plusarg or a `simulation:` knob), so the suite's transcript and the warning baselines do not change. A dividend measured on 2026-09-02: at the configured 2 Mbaud a character costs 5 us of simulated time and the host blocks on it character by character (`LSR` polling), and a green `noc` run prints 9 lines / 521 characters - about 2.6 ms of its ~9 ms, on the critical path between phases. **The constraint that shapes the design: NO hierarchical references into the tiles.** On Verilator the tiles are hierarchical blocks (`hier_block`), compiled as separate units; a dotted path from the testbench into one either fails or forces the block back inline, which is exactly the compile-time collapse the hierarchical flow exists to avoid (chapter 5). So the counters cannot be read the way memories are preloaded. Two shapes respect it: observe at the TOP, where the inter-tile links (`floo_req/rsp/wide` between routers) are plain signals - a link-activity monitor per tile side needs no visibility inside any block; and put the progress mailbox in a location the testbench already reaches with the existing preload mechanism (which must itself be checked against the hierarchical flow, not assumed). Sim-only ports on the tiles are the fallback if link observation proves insufficient, and they change a hier_block's interface between simulation and synthesis, so they are the last resort. Validate on the Verilator hierarchical flow FIRST - it is the license-free flow and the one the constraint bites - then on QuestaSim.

**PLANNED - after the CSR-programmable stamper and the progress monitors, before the nested boot: an AXI boot mode for exported macros (user proposal, 2026-09-02).** Not the nested boot, though the two share one protocol. A project exported as a macro boots today, in its own testbench, like any other project (UART, serial link, JTAG, hierarchical forces) - never the way it will be used, which is THROUGH ITS WINDOW: a parent writes the boot image into the macro's memory over its AXI slave port, ungates its domains through its system controller and hands off by writing its host's scratch registers. `testbench.boot_mode: axi` gives the macro's own testbench an AXI master agent that does exactly that, from outside the project and with no path into the design: the boundary translation (`addr - MACRO_BASE_ADDR` at the macro top) is exercised on the boot path itself, and the image lands through the same window write path silicon would use. The pass criterion stays UART + EOT: the macro's manager tile exports its host's UART, and the standalone macro projects already pass the suite on it.

What it takes: (1) the agent - `axi_test::axi_driver` from the pulp AXI package already in the graph, wrapped as a VIP agent next to the serial-link twin and the uart-boot agent, driving the macro top's AXI slave port; (2) the protocol as generated testbench sequence - image words at `window_base + boot_memory_offset` (bursts, or the load is slow), the child's system-controller writes for the gated domains, the scratch-register handoff, the same three steps the nested-boot design already lists; (3) the generator emitting the constants the agent needs, which the macro already publishes in its own headers and IP-XACT. Why before the nested boot, and why it is the approach step the user framed: the protocol gets validated on the macro ALONE, where the boundary is one clock away from the waves, and the parent's firmware then reproduces a sequence that is already known to work - the wip's own nested-boot text already foresees "its firmware (or the JTAG agent, pre-handoff)" as the loader, and this agent is that pre-handoff loader. It is also the standalone-macro boot that never reaches into a `hier_block`, so it is the one the Verilator hierarchical flow can run without the `$readmemh` accelerator.

**Wave-B preliminary analysis (2026-08-30, read-only recon).** Four facts shape the work: (1) **the integer compute unit exists upstream**: `floo_alu.sv` ships `floo_alu_top` - 64-bit, two operands, fpnew-style handshake, `CutInput/CutOutput` cuts, "designed to be used as an easy offload unit for the offload reduction" - though nothing in the IP's own `hw/` instantiates it, so ours would be the first in-tree wiring; the adapter to the router's `offload_narrow_req_o/rsp_i` structs is thin (op enum shared, format to fix at INT64/UINT64, `vector_mode` off, `tag` unused, `flush` tied). (2) **The capability perimeter is the real design decision**: `CollectiveOpDefaultCfg` is all-zeros and `universal_tile.sv.mako` passes the emitted `CollectiveCfg` only to `use_mcast` tiles' routers - so today manager, L2 and dummy routers have EVERY collective op off, multicast included, and host-originated collective traffic crosses the manager's router first. SETTLED the same night, three convergent read-only checks: floogen's own router template passes `CollectiveCfg` to EVERY router unconditionally (the upstream intent); gwaihir's cheshire/mem/dummy tiles pass nothing (the reference has our exact shape - it is the deviation's provenance, not a counter-example, and it never exercised collectives either); and the RTL is decisive - `floo_route_select.sv` with `EnMultiCast=0` ties the multicast route selection to `'0` ("No MCast supported"), so a transit router IGNORES the replication mask (degenerate route or no output at all), and `floo_router.sv` asserts `CollOpReceivedOnWrongVirtChannel` on collective flits it does not handle. **The correct perimeter is ALL routers**; the per-tile `use_mcast` conditional conflated an endpoint property (multicast_target) with a transit capability, and today's "enabled" multicast is real only inside the sixteen compute-tile routers - the manager's chimney (RouteCfgNoMcast) cannot even inject one. The narrow channel then has a uniform solution (a `floo_alu` per tile). The WIDE channel is the hard case to decide at the B kickoff: reduction computes at every merging router of the tree, and outside the compute tiles there is no FPU behind the offload - either FP ops stay enabled only in compute routers (but FP flits in TRANSIT elsewhere hit the very problem just proven), or wide reduction masks are topologically disciplined to converge on compute tiles only (documented and validated), or a minimal FP unit goes everywhere. The third option is priced out by a width fact (`floo_router.sv:274`): reduction operands are as wide as the offload channel's data width - 64 bit on the narrow, 512 bit on our wide - so a "minimal" wide unit is really an ~8-lane FP64 SIMD block per router (mantissa multipliers dominating; hundreds of kGE each, several MGE across the mesh's non-compute routers, versus tens of kGE for the scalar integer `floo_alu`). This is also why upstream offloads the wide channel to the cluster DCA, the one place an FP vector unit already exists. The realistic wide options therefore merge into one: FP ops enabled only on compute routers AND wide reduction masks disciplined - with a generation-time refusal - to trees that never merge outside them; MAGIA's configuration (six integer ops on, four FP off) implicitly makes the same call. (3) **Software injects reductions through the AXI user bits**: the chimney reads `user.collective_op` when configured (`floo_nw_chimney.sv`, otherwise tied to zero - the ENUMVALUE-suppression site), and the multicast mask travels in the address; the schema's `UserMapping` (today `amo_msb/lsb`, `ecc_err_bit`) must grow a `collective_op` allocation and the mesh's user width must fit it. (4) Only after the plumbing exists does the MAGIA optimization matrix (-O5/-permissive vs +acc, plus the Verilator data point) become testable here. Shape: B1 plumbing + perimeter (pilot on `noc`), B2 user-mapping + injection, B3 the offload-app functional test (multicast payload, barrier sync, reduction collect, multicast-aware TB monitors, generator-side expected values), one full NoC+super gate, ONE commit together with the frozen wave A. The natural vehicle is the offload test - multicast the payload to the sixteen clusters in one transaction instead of sixteen, synchronise on the hardware barrier, and collect the results by reduction - which would also turn our own four enabled-but-untested features into exercised ones. What needs care is the testbench: its AXI monitors must read a multicast transaction as such and not as a decode error.

### 3.7 Audit of the Dependency Registry's Patches and Pre-Build Commands

The registry has accumulated its repairs incrementally — 14 inline patch triples and 11 pre-build commands as of 2026-08-07, plus the per-IP scripts they invoke — and each was added against the toolchain of its day. Several are suspected to be historical: the Verilator 5.041→5.050 bump alone dissolved a whole class of workarounds, and the 2026-08-06 manifest cleanups changed what even reaches the compilers. A stale patch is not just dead weight: it documents a defect that may no longer exist, misleading the next reader.

The audit is a TRIAGE first and experiments second — revalidating everything experimentally would be the wrong instrument, because the cost of one experiment depends on which tool the entry serves: a QuestaSim-only repair revalidates in a ~50-minute suite pass, but an entry that exists FOR Verilator (the packed-ification above all) needs the dual-backend leg, which measured 5h10 on just `crossbar noc`. So, per entry, classify on documentary evidence before touching anything:

1.  **Surely necessary**: a probe or failure on the *current* pins and tools stands behind it (the hci self-assignments and the packed-ification, probed on 5.050 in the 2026-08-05/06 campaign, are here by construction). No experiment needed; make sure the rationale comment names the failing configuration precisely enough to be re-verified next time.
2.  **Doubtful**: added against tools or revisions that have since moved, with no recent evidence either way. Only this category gets experiments — ordered by cost (Questa-only first), batched only when the entries are independent enough that a failure still bisects cheaply.
3.  **Surely useless**: the target file or IP no longer in the graph, the search string no longer matching, or the defect verifiably fixed at the current pin. Drop without experiments, keeping the removal in git history rather than as a commented relic. Note that the patch engine already emits a free detector for this class: the "Stale patch" WARNING it prints when a search no longer matches a freshly restored file — the generation logs of a full suite pass are the first place to harvest candidates.

Most entries demonstrably work for a living — `cv64a6_imafdc_sv39` selects the CVA6 core variant among the many `target: cv64a6_*` branches of its manifest, `cv32e40p_use_ff_regfile` selects the register-file implementation in two manifests, `idma`/`use_idma`/`snitch_cluster`/`spatz`/`deprecated` gate files, and `idma`/`spatz` are additionally tested as `ifdef TARGET_*` in sources. Verification method: drop one at a time, regenerate and fast-check (the grep pass is a screen, not proof — nested `all(...)`/`any(...)` manifest expressions and per-family graphs differ, so probe against both topology families before removing anything).

#### Idea recorded: a `// BENDER_TARGET:` pragma for the isles

Many surviving targets are semantically a **component's** requirement, not a flow's: the cva6 variant targets belong to `cheshire_isle`, the spatz/snitch ones to `spatz_cluster_isle`. The isle already declares its dependencies (`// BENDER:`) and its compile-time symbols (`// DEFINE:`); a third pragma declaring the bender targets its wrapped IP needs would complete the component's compilation contract and let the registry keep only what it is actually for — version homogeneity across the SoC. Same propagation rules as the existing two (inherited through macros); the union still reaches `bender script` unchanged, so the mechanics are collection-side only. Worth doing together with the target audit above, so what survives the audit is what gets migrated.

#### Dependency override: the fleet witness is still missing (delivered 2026-08-26, f3ec2aa)

A project's dependency entry now replaces the catalogue's wholly, and what is not inherited is announced in the generation log; the semantics live in the environment guide's dependency section. **The residual is the witness**: no example project declares `dependencies` in its `*_env.yml`, so the replacement path is exercised by nothing and a green suite attests only the catalogue-only path. A project that permanently overrides a dependency carrying patches and pre-build commands (so replacement and the announcement line are both covered) remains to be added.

#### Hierarchical preload of the host's INTERNAL scratchpad: not available, and the reference agrees

Cheshire's internal SPM is not a flat SRAM: it IS the last-level cache, whose ways can each be switched between caching and scratchpad duty (`docs/um/arch.md`: "On initial boot, all ways are configured as SPM"), and `SizeSpm = get_llc_size(cfg)` says as much. So the storage of a `$readmemh` target would be `axi_llc_ways.gen_data_ways[j].i_data_sram`, addressed through axi_llc's own way/set decomposition - a `PreloadTemplate` string like `l2_isle`'s cannot express it without reimplementing a third-party IP's internal mapping in our generator. Architected preloads (jtag/slink/uart) have no such problem: they write by ADDRESS, the LLC dispatches, and no dotted path is needed - which also means the host tile keeps its Verilator hierarchical-block status.

gwaihir reached the same conclusion twice. Its backdoor "fast mode" (PRELMODE=3) long carried `$fatal("Cheshire memory region not supported yet")` next to a TODO; when the cost finally forced the issue (#84, 2026-08-20: "host bring-up still bit-banged the whole ELF over the debug module -- tens of minutes"), the fix was NOT a hierarchical write into the SPM but PER-SECTION ROUTING - sections landing in the L2 tiles go through the backdoor, every other section, the SPM included, still goes over JTAG. That hybrid is worth remembering for the multi-sim wave's fast-preload mode: back-door where the storage layout is linear, architected transport everywhere else, decided section by section.

#### Boot memory executability: the symmetric check is still missing (refusal delivered 2026-08-25, 950acd1)

A boot memory outside every region the host can execute from now stops generation, naming the address and the windows it missed (configuration guide, chapter 7). **The residual is the symmetric case**: a boot memory that IS executable but not cached, where the host fetches correctly and every `fence.i`-published payload is stale. The generator holds the terms (the CIE window, the LLC-backed regions, the boot memory's resolved window); the check belongs next to the delivered one.

#### Heterogeneous memory tiles: DELIVERED, with one residual (2026-08-27)

**The behaviour now lives in the user documentation**: `base_addr` and `size_per_instance` accept a scalar or a per-instance list, the four layouts and the instance-order contract are `soc_configuration_guide.md` section 3.1, and the length check is section 7.7. `mesh` is the fleet's witness - a uniform 1 MB stride with alternating 1 MB / 512 KB mapped depth, the shallow tiles leaving the upper half of their slot unmapped, exactly the reference design's pattern at our scale. What follows is only what is NOT done.

**The waste this entry used to record is gone, and the question that removed it is worth keeping.** The first cut had `mesh`'s shallow tiles mapping 512 KB while still holding 1 MB of SRAM, on the grounds that `MemSize` lives in the component's `parameters` block and one value serves every instance. The right objection - why two fields at all, when a window and a depth should be the same number? - showed that the pair is only meaningful in ONE direction: a window LARGER than the storage is ordinary (an aligned slot bigger than what fills it), while a window SMALLER than the storage is silicon nobody can address. The fix was not per-instance parameters but removing the pin: `sram_isle` already declares `MemSize = InstanceWindowSize`, and the generator preserves that dependency through the tile wrapper, so dropping `MemSize` from the project YAML makes each tile build exactly the depth it maps. What made it bite was decoupling the two halves of the identity convention, which had been coupled by accident - the window was handed only to components that also asked for a base, and a memory decoding on low address bits asks for neither.

**Residual: parameters other than the window still cannot vary per instance.** A component whose instances need different values for something that is NOT derivable from the window - a FIFO depth, a core count - still has no way to say so, since `parameters` is per component. No shipped example needs it, which is why it stays a note rather than a plan; the shape it would take is the one the address fields now use, a list resolved once and applied in instance order. The reference design's two-module split (`mem_tile_small` / `mem_tile_large`) is the alternative we deliberately do not follow: one module parameterized per instance is the better shape, at the cost of one verilated variant per distinct value.

Two things learned while delivering it, both worth keeping:

*   **An external format that expresses an array as one stride needs UNROLLING, and there were two of them.** FlooGen's `array:` and RDL's `name[N] @ base += stride` have the identical limitation, and took the identical remedy: one declaration per instance when a list breaks uniformity. Neither reaches our modules - a tile receives its identity as a coordinate (`id_t`), and nothing outside FlooGen's own package names an endpoint - so the unrolling changes address maps and never interfaces. Any third generator fed from these fields will need the same treatment.
*   **Normalise once, immediately after validation.** Some twenty places legitimately want one integer ("where does this component start"), and teaching each of them four layouts would have got one of them wrong. Instead the lists are resolved in `normalize_address_ranges` and the entry is rewritten to carry both: `_windows` for the per-instance truth, and instance 0's values under the original names so that every existing consumer keeps working untouched. That is what made the change a strict addition - the whole fleet regenerated byte-identically before `mesh` itself was aligned.

#### The intermittent offload stall: not reproduced since the toolchain fixes (downgraded 2026-08-29)

Three unequal-depth runs of `mesh` once ended in `FAILED (UART output but no EOT)`, in configurations that were not recorded well enough to reproduce; every controlled run since has passed. Two structural causes were found and fixed in the meantime, both capable of producing exactly this shape of failure: **stale simulation libraries** (`build-sim` now rebuilds `work/` unconditionally) and **object-cache poisoning** (ccache 3.x with Verilator's pch; the build now disables the pch below ccache 4). Since those fixes the two-depth configuration - now the fleet's per-instance-depth witness in `mesh.yml` - has been green across three full gates on both simulators, offload to EOT every time. The entry stays only as a recognizer: **if a run again prints UART and never reaches EOT with no error, suspect the build products before the RTL** - rebuild `work/` and the Verilator model from scratch with the sanctioned cache, and only a failure that survives that rebuild is a design lead. No per-run snapshot, no claim: record the exact `mesh.yml`, the library state and the cache state with any future specimen.

#### Reset, clock/reset control per instance, and isolation - DELIVERED, with three residuals (2026-08-28)

The wave planned on 2026-08-27 is done and the behaviour it produced is documented where a user will find it: the reset convention in [clocking, reset and CDC requirements](../../hw/clocking_reset_cdc_requirements.md) section 2.3, the two-reset and isolation contracts in [component standardization](../../hw/component_standardization.md) section 4, `power_on_state` / the write-ordering contract / the group bit index / refusal 7.8 in the [SoC configuration guide](../../soc_configuration_guide.md). Only what is **not** done stays here.

**1. The software-reset paths are not declared false, because we ship no constraints.** The design rule that justifies the missing synchronizer — a software reset is written only while the target's clock is gated, so recovery and removal have no meaning on it — is enforced today only by the firmware helpers' write order. It becomes a *timing* requirement the moment an ASIC flow exists, and the first synthesis run will otherwise try to close a path we deliberately chose not to close. Whoever lands the constraints owns this; the requirement is stated in the clocking document so it cannot be discovered by surprise.

**2. Nothing validates a reset release, at any gate size.** RTL simulation does not check recovery and removal, so a release a cycle early is invisible to the whole suite. The wave's answer was to maximise the *number* of bring-ups instead (the generated `offload` application power-cycles each target and brings its instances up one at a time), which is stress rather than proof. Real coverage needs either gate-level simulation with SDF or a formal reset check — both out of reach until the ASIC flow lands, and both worth revisiting then rather than inventing a substitute now.

**3. The inbound fence does not exist, and is deliberately not `isolate`.** Isolation is outbound in every RTL instance we have, ours and the references': it protects the network from the block. Nothing stops a transaction *arriving* at a block that is gated, and not addressing a parked block is therefore a firmware responsibility (the generated phases never touch a target they have parked). Should the protection ever be wanted, the shape is a tile-level cell on the always-on clock with `TerminateTransaction`, and it is a **new function needing its own name** — widening `isolate` to mean both directions would make the existing flag ambiguous in every project that already sets it.

#### The memory isles name the same things differently (censused 2026-08-27)

**Observation from the user, and the census confirms it**: across the three memory isles, three roles carry two names each, while the one role that HAS a convention is uniform everywhere.

| role | `sram_isle` | `spm_isle` | `l2_isle` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| window in the address map | `InstanceWindowSize` | `InstanceWindowSize` | `InstanceWindowSize` |
| depth of the storage | `MemSize` | `SpmTileSize` | *(none - derived)* |
| bank data width | `SramDataWidth` | `SpmDataWidth` | *(fixed at 32, a localparam)* |
| words per bank | `SramNumWords` | `SpmWordsPerBank` | *(derived)* |

Project descriptions reference the fragmented names 24 times across the fleet (`SpmWordsPerBank` and `SpmDataWidth` eight each, the `Sram*` pair four each).

**Where the fragmentation is tells the story.** `InstanceWindowSize` is identical in all three because the identity convention (`docs/hw/component_standardization.md` section 1.6) named it once and every isle opted in. The divergence sits precisely where no convention exists - the physical geometry of the banks - and each isle named it for itself.

**So the target is not a rename.** `l2_isle` declares none of the three and derives them from the window plus its own internals, which is the proof that a memory isle need not ask a project for its bank geometry at all. The question worth answering is *which parameters a project should be obliged to declare*: the good end state is a description that states a window and stays silent, with geometry derived or defaulted per technology, not four names reduced to two. A pure rename would tidy the surface and leave the redundancy.

Two things already resolved, and they are the pattern to follow: `MemSize` and `SpmTileSize` both default to `InstanceWindowSize`, so a project that does not pin them gets "build exactly what you map" for free - `mesh.yml` stopped declaring `MemSize` on 2026-08-27, and any project declaring `SpmTileSize` can do the same.

**Cost and sequencing.** The isles are ours to change (`components/isles/`), but the names appear in every example description, so this is a user-visible migration: it deserves its own wave and its own commit, announceable and revertible on its own. It must NOT ride along with a semantic change - a mechanical rename and a feature in one gate means two candidate causes when the gate turns red.

#### Registry policy: four entries still float by semver (FlooGen alignment delivered 2026-08-27, eef4684)

FlooGen and FlooNoC are pinned to the same 0.8.4 (`requirements.txt` and an `overrides` revision), and a coherence check runs before FlooGen does, scoped to `topology.type == "noc"`. **What stays open is the registry policy**: `apb`, `apb_adv_timer`, `mailbox_unit` and `timer_unit` still resolve by semver constraint, so two clones of one Ollivander commit can fetch different revisions (`Bender.lock` is git-ignored). On stable libraries floating is defensible; deciding per entry - and pinning the ones whose emitted-code/library coupling matters - is the task.

### 3.8 A User-Configurable Address Map for `l2_isle`

The dyn_mem-based `l2_isle` (crossbar family only: the mesh memories are `sram_isle`/`spm_isle` and flat) inherits astral's dual-view address plan: the lower half of the component window is the interleaved view of the banks, the upper half is the NON-interleaved view of the SAME physical words (`dyn_mem_addr_map.sv` re-encodes the low address bits and consumes the view-selection bit). Discovered 2026-08-07 by the offload test, whose payload written in the "upper half" physically overwrote the host image (the two views collide word-by-word: linear group-1 row N is interleaved address `base + N*16 + 8`). With the current plan, of the 2 MB of physical storage 512 KB are double-mapped and 512 KB (the upper rows of group 0) are unreachable from the crossbar window. The offload payload region now avoids the issue by construction (second quarter of the window, same view as the image - rtl_generator.py records the rationale), but the map itself remains a semantic decision the user never made.

**Decision (2026-08-07): the map must be user-configurable per component.** The translation functions are hardwired in the dyn_mem IP (`NUM_MAP_TYPES = 2`), but the address plan is entirely the wrapper's: `MappingRules` is a localparam of `l2_isle.sv` handed to `mapping_rules_i`. The plan is therefore a wrapper `parameter` away from being a YAML decision, e.g. `L2AddrMapMode = "dual_view"` (default, astral-compatible, today's behaviour) or `"flat"` (a single INTERLEAVE rule spanning the whole window: full capacity usable, no aliasing, no dark region - the passthrough already keeps enough row bits).

*   **Advantages**: replicates astral's per-buffer view choice when wanted; gives every other design a flat RAM with the full declared capacity; removes the trap the offload test fell into at its root; zero IP patches.
*   **Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies**: the interleaved preload must be validated over the full row range in "flat" mode (split_hex mapping is row-count agnostic, but the testbench paths must be exercised); the generated payload carve and the documentation of `boot_memory` sizing should follow the selected mode (the generator can read the component's `parameters`); hello_world's stack placement (end of window) must be re-checked in both modes. Schedule after the offload phase 1 closes, so the two validations do not interleave.

### 3.9 Instance Identity: Residuals of the L2 Fold

The instance identity convention (`docs/hw/component_standardization.md` section 2.6) is the single per-instance window mechanism since 2026-08-11. Two residuals:

*   **Macro export: window-access contract, force-free ladder first (designed with the user, 2026-08-11; deliberately AFTER the force-free bring-up work of section 2.1).** Today the two families diverge (crossbar macros keep GLOBAL addresses inside via `MACRO_BASE_ADDR + base`; NoC macros rebase to LOCAL at their border adapters) and no test ever crosses a macro window. The agreed ladder, in order:
    1. **Window plumbing check.** The exporting generator emits a `NestedAccessMemOffs`/`NestedAccessMemSize` contract block in the macro top's header, pointing at an always-on memory it selects itself (the mesh SPMs; a macro with nothing always-on emits no block, which is itself contract information). The parent discovers targets through `get_isle_info` exactly like `Offload*` - no hand-typed YAML - and the offload app gains a phase: address-derived pattern write, read back, compare, `[NESTED] PASS/FAIL`, EOT policy unchanged. Covers both export kinds symmetrically (isle macro in super_crossbar, subtile macro in super_noc). One map calibration required: super_crux's `LlcOutRegion` must shrink to the hyperbus backing store ([0x8000_0000, +128 MiB)), or the parent CVA6 reads the window back from its own cache and the test lies (same CIE/LlcOut discipline as the mesh examples).
    2. **Contract harmonization**: macro tops declare `InstanceBaseAddr`/`InstanceWindowSize` like any self-mapping isle (`MACRO_BASE_ADDR` renamed, the special case in `rtl_ir_builder.py` deleted; macro ARRAYS inherit the per-instance route for free).
    3. **Symmetric implementation, LOCAL inside both families**: the crossbar macro top gains the same boundary remap the NoC border adapters already have (an adder on the AW/AR slave path, cuttable for ASIC timing), every identity parameter inside a macro goes back to project-local, and relocation arithmetic leaves the generator entirely. Suspicion to verify with the step-1 test in place: crux_isle's internal xbar rules may be local while its identity params were global, i.e. the crossbar macro may already be broken for a nonzero base. Outgoing-traffic semantics documented once for both families: local addresses for a macro's own ranges, parent-global passthrough for external targets.
    3. **Nested boot reusing the sibling's own generated firmware** - the parent acts as loader and bring-up agent through the window, with REAL writes (the nested Cheshire's scratch registers are inside the window: the passive-boot protocol needs no forces at all). Three constraints clarified with the user: (a) firmware reuse REQUIRES step 3 first - the image is linked on the nested LOCAL map, position-independent only under the local-inside convention (today's crossbar-global macro would decode it wrong); (b) the firmware BYTES do not belong in the SV shell nor in a simulation filelist: the shell carries the address contract (boot memory offset, scratch block, entry) while the bytes travel in the sibling's `generated/sw` artifact bundle, consumed by the parent's software build like an offload payload (bin2header), with a freshness check between image and RTL; (c) the macro tile must first WIRE what it ties to '0 today - the nested `pwr_on_rst_ni` (from a parent control group) and `rt_clk_i` - or the nested host cannot run at all; a clean completion observable (nested Cheshire scratch[2] via `_exit`, or a nested sys-ctrl scratch) also needs picking. Step 4 is the end-to-end validation of every exported macro, and doubles as the cross-boundary proof of the force-free bring-up mechanics of section 2.1 - which is why the whole ladder queues BEHIND that work.

*   **Exotic parameter types still fall back to value inference** in the tile wrapper (package-scoped types are deliberately excluded from the declared-type pass-through). Harmless today; revisit only if an isle ever declares a pkg-typed value parameter the tile must re-expose.

### 3.10 Consuming spatz Through Its Own Meta-Generation (clustergen)

*Priority raised on 2026-08-17 (user decision): instantiating an IP the way its author prescribes is a supportability prerequisite - upstream issues and PRs are only proposable from the intended consumption flow. This entry moves ahead of the rest of chapter 3.*

*Field analysis done the same day against `spatz_cluster.carfield.l2.hjson` (the project-hjson precedent: Astral's `spatzd-hw-init` builds spatz exactly this way). Everything carfield changes versus the stock hjson splits cleanly: the INTEGRATION section is entirely computable by Ollivander today - `cluster_base_addr` (component's axi_slave base), `cluster_base_hartid` (hart-id policy), `addr/data_width` (global_bus), `id_width_in/out` (the published ID-width derivation), `user_width` + default user, `axi_cdc_enable` (clock_domain), `sw_rst_enable`/`axi_isolate_enable` (the isle contract's own features), and the `dram {address, length}` target region (the SoC map - the exact `tcdm_start` bug class the patches repair today); `dma_data_width` tracks `data_width` in carfield, so it is likely integration too. What remains is a small microarchitectural set (`cores[]` ISA/extensions/outstanding/TLB, `vlen`, TCDM and icache geometry, `buf_fpu`) whose natural home is the component's free-form `parameters` block, merged by the generator over a stock-hjson default. The plumbing hook already exists: the registry's spatz `pre_build_cmds` switches from a stock hjson to the generated one, same pattern as Astral.*

spatz is designed to be consumed like snitch_cluster: its RTL, wrapper and BOOTROM are generated from an hjson configuration by the IP's own flow (the repo ships per-integration configs, carfield included - astral regenerates from `spatz_cluster.carfield.l2.hjson` instead of patching). Ollivander instead consumes the checked-in default-config artifacts and adapts at the boundary, which is where the bootrom patches of patch_spatz.py come from. Proposed 2026-08-10: evaluate moving spatz to pre_build meta-generation, the way the snitch_cluster registry entry already runs `make rtl`.

**SCOPE CORRECTED BY MEASUREMENT (the paragraph above overstated it - read this one first).** Three facts, each verified in source, shrink this task to a fraction of what it looked like and remove its main risk:

1.  **The generated wrapper is NOT the integration API, and for spatz it is not even adoptable.** `spatz_cluster_wrapper.sv` BAKES the cluster identity as constants (`.hart_base_id_i (10'h0)`, `.cluster_base_addr_i (32'h100000)`), so one wrapper serves exactly one instance at one base - unusable for an array. Its only consumers inside the IP are the standalone testharness, its testbench template and the lint script. snitch's generated wrapper is the opposite (`input logic [9:0] hart_base_id_i`, `input logic [47:0] cluster_base_addr_i`), which is why `cluster_base_expose: true` is patched into snitch's cfg and why `cluster_subtile.sv` CAN instantiate it. The asymmetry between the two families in our tree is therefore imposed by the IPs, not a legacy inconsistency to unify.
2.  **Astral does the same thing we do.** It hand-writes `hw/spatz_cluster_wrapper.sv` (tracked in its own git) around `spatz_cluster #(...)`, and its `spatzd-hw-init` runs `make -B SPATZ_CLUSTER_CFG=.../cfg/carfield.hjson **bootrom**` - the `generate` target is never invoked. Our `spatz_cluster_isle.sv` is the exact analogue. gwaihir does not instantiate spatz at all, so there is no second opinion to weigh.
3.  **The whole value is the BOOTROM.** `test/bootdata_bootrom.cc` is a symlink into `src/generated/`, rendered by clustergen from the hjson, and it bakes `tcdm_start` <- `cluster_base_addr`, `hartid_base`, `core_count` <- `len(cores[])`, `tcdm_size`, `tcdm_offset` and `global_mem_start/end` <- `dram{address,length}`. `tcdm_start` is literally the field patch_spatz.py repairs. Downstream consumption needs NO change: `src/generated/bootrom.sv` is already in the Bender manifest and already reached through `i_axi_to_reg_bootrom` inside the `spatz_cluster` we instantiate - the same file we compile today is simply written with our values instead of the IP's defaults.

The fields the generated hjson must therefore carry: the integration set (`cluster_base_addr`, `cluster_base_hartid`, `cluster_base_offset`, `addr_width`, `data_width`, `user_width`, `id_width_in/out`, `cluster_default_axi_user`, `axi_cdc_enable`, `dma_data_width`, plus `axi_isolate_enable`/`sw_rst_enable` which are ABSENT from the stock config and must be added), the `dram{address,length}` target region (the stock `[0x8000_0000, +2G)` misses our L2, exactly the defect class the snitch cfg patch repairs), the `cores[]` array LENGTH replicated to the instantiated core count (a structural edit, not a scalar one), and `tcdm.size`, which our isle mirrors by hand as `OffloadCtrlOffs`. Microarchitecture (`vlen`, `n_fpu`, `spatz_nports`, `buf_fpu`, `timing.*`, `icache.*`) stays as the base config has it.

**PILOT RESULT (crux, offload app): `[OFFLOAD] spatz_cluster PASS` with the bootrom generated from this project's own configuration and NO patch left on the ROM.** Both bootrom repairs are gone, and gone structurally rather than by luck: with `tcdm_start` correct, the IP's ORIGINAL entry sequence derives the entry-point register itself (`bootrom.S` computes `tcdm_start + tcdm_size` = the cluster peripherals base, then reaches `CLUSTER_BOOT_CONTROL`), and with `dma_data_width` equal to the SoC data width the ROM is emitted 64 bits wide (`addr_i[AddrBits-1+3:3]`, word-addressed) so nothing truncates on the way out. What survives in patch_spatz.py is only the `spatz_cc.sv` tracer repair, which lives in a NON-generated file and no configuration can reach.

Two frictions the pilot paid for, both invisible from the documentation: (1) **the IP's pinned riscv-opcodes revision no longer exists upstream** - `sw/toolchain/riscv-opcodes.version` names a commit present in NO branch of pulp-platform/riscv-opcodes, so the vendor's own bootrom flow cannot run as shipped; the registry re-pins it to a live revision by patching that file; upstream refreshed the version itself meanwhile (#119, past our pin), so a future spatz bump retires the patch. (2) the opcodes generator calls bare `python`, absent on this host class, so the venv's bin goes on PATH for that one step.

`boot_addr` is deliberately NOT driven, and this is a measurement rather than an omission: in the RTL it only reaches the GENERATED WRAPPER (`.BootAddr (32'h1000)`), which nothing instantiates - our isle passes `BootAddr = 32'h5103_0000` straight to `spatz_cluster` - and in software it is BOOTDATA offset 0, which `bootrom.S` never loads (it reads offsets 24 and 32 only). carfield leaves it at `0x1000` too while placing its cluster elsewhere. Driving it would add a key to the drift surface, and a misleading trace for the next reader, in exchange for nothing.

Two constraints for the implementation, both able to fail silently: (a) `patches` run BEFORE `pre_build_cmds` and `make bootrom` REWRITES `bootrom.sv`, so patch_spatz.py must run AFTER the regeneration or the PC-relative repair is erased; (b) the isle passes `BootAddr = 32'h5103_0000` (a fixed offset from the peripherals, which the PC-relative sequence requires) while the hjson carries its own `boot_addr` - the two must agree, or the double truth reopens in a new form. Also note `make bootrom` has the wrapper among its prerequisites, so the wrapper IS regenerated even though nothing instantiates it: it stays in the manifest, so it must still compile - a pilot check, not a detail.

The consistent endpoint would be Ollivander GENERATING the spatz hjson from the SoC YAML (cluster base, TCDM geometry, core count) exactly as it generates the FlooGen configuration - single source, no drift.

#### Advantages
*   **Structurally removes the baked-assumption class**: a bootrom generated for the project's own base cannot disagree with it; the BOOTDATA patch family disappears for the single-instance case.
*   **Architectural consistency**: one consumption model for the whole snitch family (snitch_cluster already meta-generates in our flow).
*   **Config authority**: the isle's hardwired geometry (base, TCDM, PMA regions) could derive from the same generated config instead of being hand-mirrored localparams.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **The PC-relative patch is stronger than any regenerated base**: a baked tcdm_start serves ONE base, while crux-style SoCs may want N instances (the very reason the patch went position-independent). Regeneration alone regresses that.
    *   *Mitigation*: keep the PC-relative entry sequence as a patch (or upstream it) even under meta-generation; regeneration then only carries geometry.
*   **The sub-line slicing repair is width-related, not config-related**: the ROM serves 512-bit lines regardless of hjson, and our isle runs the bus at 64. Unclear that any config regenerates it away.
    *   *Mitigation*: verify against the spatz generation flow before promising; the slicing patch (or its upstream fix) may stay regardless.
*   **Pre-build toolchain dependency**: the bootrom build needs a riscv32 GCC at generation time (the snitch_cluster pre_build already carries a comparable burden - pip, llvm symlinks).
    *   *Mitigation*: same pattern, same registry entry style; the toolchain is already a documented prerequisite of the offload app.

### 3.11 Per-Tile Clock Scaling on the NoC: Gating Exists, Scaling Does Not (analysed 2026-08-26)

A mesh has exactly two functional clocks - `clk_i` and `rt_clk_i` - and every component sits in the `system` domain (`generators: 0`, two domains). Each tile does receive a **clock enable** (`tile_clk_en_i` feeding a `tc_clk_gating`), so a tile can be turned OFF, which the offload test already exercises through its power-cycle phase. What a tile cannot do is run SLOWER: there is no per-tile divider, and no YAML can create one, because `clock_domain` does not appear in the tile template at all.

The capability is not missing from the generator: the crossbar family exercises it on eight domains, and its generated top carries `spatz_clk_muxed` / `spatz_clk` with the comment "Final gated/divided clock", plus the mux, divider and control registers that come with it. The mesh simply does not use it. gwaihir does not either - one functional clock at its top, and the only `clk_en` signals are the PCIe test ones - so this is a feature neither side has, not a divergence.

**Three ways to put a tile in its own domain, and two of them are wrong.** Worth recording all three, because the wrong ones are what gets tried first.

| where the crossing goes | who must provide it | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| router to router | FlooNoC | not needed, and no async link support in the RTL we compile |
| isle to chimney, INSIDE the tile | us - and the isles already expose it | **the right one** |
| a different clock routed to the whole tile | nobody | broken by construction |

The third is broken because **chimney and router both live inside the tile** (verified: one of each in `mesh_compute_clusters_tile.sv`, none in the top). Feeding the tile a different clock would move the router too, and its links to the neighbouring routers would become unmanaged domain crossings - which is precisely the case that would need FlooNoC's async support.

The second is available because the isles already do this for the other family: all five crossbar isles instantiate `axi_cdc`, and what they publish is already an asynchronous FIFO-based AXI bus (`async_axi_out_aw_data_o`, `..._wptr_o`, `..._rptr_i`). The isle carries its own half of the crossing. The tile template also already knows how to close such a bus against a clock - the `pfx.startswith("async_")` branch with `axi_cdc_dst`, used today for the macro boundary and the error paths.

So the work is composition, not invention: the tile takes TWO clocks (the network's and its domain's), instantiates the isle on the latter, uses the async branch towards the chimney, and the mesh's `clock_tree` starts declaring generators and per-tile domains the way `crux.yml` already does. The control registers come for free from the system controller. What needs real attention is the testbench: the memory backdoors and the AXI monitors reach into tiles that would now sit in another domain.

**Status, 2026-09-04: the crossing exists, the scaling still does not.** The wire-controlled wave gave the universal tile the isle-to-chimney crossing this section called the right place - `axi_cdc_src` / `axi_cdc_dst` on the network clock against an isle that publishes its own half (`pulp_cluster_isle` on `noc_isle`) - but closed on ONE clock: the tile still takes a single `clk_i`, gated by its control-group bit, and the network side is that same clock. What remains is the scaling proper: (1) a second clock input on the tile, the domain's own, muxed and divided by a `clock_tree` that the mesh examples do not declare yet (one `system` domain today; `crux.yml` shows the registers and the generator's side); (2) the SYNC isles - the Snitch subtile, the memory isles - have no crossing at all and would need a full `axi_cdc` pair per data path inside the tile, where the async isles need only the counterpart half; (3) the testbench's memory backdoors and AXI monitors, which reach into tiles that would then sit in another domain. The dividend is real - the PULP bridge pays its latency today for nothing - but the wave is still the size the analysis above gave it.

### 3.13 Shrinking the Crossbar Family's `LlcOutRegion` (deferred 2026-08-24, autonomous-boot wave)

In `crux`, `crux_isle` and `super_crux`, cheshire's `LlcOutRegion` still spans `[0x8000_0000, 0x1_0000_0000)` while the memory backing it (the hyperbus behind `llc_port`) covers only `[0x8000_0000, 0x8800_0000)`. The excess 1.9 GiB keeps cheshire-internal decode shadowing alive for anything mapped inside it - today, `super_crux`'s nested mesh macro window at `0x9000_0000` - and marks unbacked space cached and executable for CVA6 (`LlcOutRegionStart/End` also feed `ExecuteRegion`/`CachedRegion`). The L2 windows themselves already left the region during the autonomous-boot wave (moved into the CIE window at `0x7800_0000`, astral's own base and shape); this entry is the leftover half of that resolution.

The fix is `super_mesh`'s own practice: shrink `LlcOutRegionEnd` to the hyperbus end, as the isle comment already instructs. Verify cheshire tolerates the shrunk window, and re-place the `super_crux` macro window if the shadow is to be closed before the shrink. Detection context: the shadowing was exposed by `addr_decode_dync`'s one-hot `ASSERT_FINAL`, which fires on any bare `make run-sim` whose `$finish` freezes a shadowed address on the AR channel - and never in the suite, which runs `ASSERTIONS=0`; our own overlap check is section 4.5. Optional upstream question, if ever filed: is an `AxiExt` region inside `LlcOutRegion` supported at all, given that assert?

## 4. Input Validation: What Is Still Missing

Ollivander refuses malformed input rather than absorbing it, and what it does refuse is documented for users in section 7 of `docs/soc_configuration_guide.md`. Three gaps remain.

### 4.1 The Remaining By-Name References

`clock_domain` and `software_stack.boot_memory` are resolved against what the description declares. Three other references are not, each for a reason that has to be decided before it can be checked:

*   The component and port named in an `interrupts` source (`"mailbox.snd_irq_o[4]"`). The port half is already caught, fatally, when the wiring resolves it against the module; the *component* half is not, and a source may deliberately be `none`, or a bitwise expression over several components.
*   The hierarchical `instance` of a `testbench.preload_memories` entry. It names RTL hierarchy that the generator only partly knows — a path inside an external IP is legitimate and unverifiable at generation time.
*   The `target` of a `macro_settings` export (`"[9,3].East"` for NoC, `"host"` for crossbar). A malformed coordinate is caught downstream; one that is well formed but names a router outside the grid is not.

*Decision to take*: for each, whether a reference that cannot be resolved is an error or a legitimately deferred one. The preload path is the clearest case for leaving it alone; the interrupt source component is the clearest case for checking it.

### 4.2 `` `default_nettype none `` in Generated Sources

An independent safety net: it would turn any undeclared signal into an elaboration error instead of an implicit wire. Attractive because it catches a whole family of mistakes by their *consequence* rather than their cause — the floating clock that motivated the validation work would have been caught by this too, from the other end.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **It may surface implicit nets that already exist**, in generated code or in the hand-written components, and each would have to be judged rather than mechanically fixed.
    *   *Mitigation*: add the directive to one generated file at a time, starting with the SoC top-level, and run the fast-check after each — it is the cheapest gate that sees elaboration.

### 4.3 Pydantic Models for the Free-Form Blocks

`interfaces`, `system_config`, `features`, `placement`, `dedicated_clock_div`, `testbench` and `software_stack` are validated by a shape specification in `soc_schema.py` while remaining plain dictionaries at run time. Turning them into real models would remove that parallel specification, but it validates nothing more, so this is internal cleanup rather than a correctness matter.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **The consumer surface is large**: 123 dictionary-style accesses of `interfaces` alone, across Python and Mako, plus the equivalent for the other blocks.
    *   *Mitigation*: worth doing only alongside a refactor that already touches those consumers — the SV-IR extension of chapter 1 is the natural occasion.

### 4.4 The Package Auto-Importer Reads Comments

`auto_import_sv_packages` (the post-render pass that inserts `import <pkg>::*;` for every `pkg::symbol` the rendered file uses) does not strip comments first: on 2026-08-21 a testbench comment mentioning a C++ exception in `pkg::symbol` spelling earned the generated bench a spurious built-in-package import. Mitigated by rewording the template comment (with a warning note for future editors); the real fix is masking comments and string literals before the scan — small, but it touches a pass every generated `.sv` goes through, so it deserves its own validated change rather than a ride-along.

The flow itself landed on 2026-08-05: `make build-sim-verilator` / `run-sim-verilator` build and run the generated testbench with no simulator license, validated end-to-end on the `noc` example with UART output and EOT byte-identical to a QuestaSim run of the same tree. Usage, costs and toolchain requirements are user documentation now (`docs/getting_started.md`, section 8.3); the defects found in external IPs along the way are in [`upstream_pr_candidates.md`](upstream_pr_candidates.md). This chapter tracks only what remains open.
### 4.5 Address Overlap: Covered by Two Third-Party Tools, by Neither of Ours (measured 2026-08-26)

Ollivander has NO address-overlap check. What it has is `noc_placement_checker`, which validates that two components do not claim the same mesh CELL - a different property. Overlap is nonetheless caught today, and the two experiments that establish it are worth recording because the answer differs by family:

*   **NoC**: FlooGen refuses it at phase 5. Two SPM windows deliberately overlapped in `mesh.yml` produced `Value error, Overlapping ranges: [60000000:60040000] and [60020000:60060000]`, exit 1.
*   **crossbar**: PeakRDL refuses it at phase 6, and with a better message - it names the components, not only the ranges: `Instance 'spatz_cluster' at offset +0x50400000:0x50BFFFFF overlaps with 'pulp_cluster' at offset +0x50000000:0x507FFFFF`. Every component window reaches the generated RDL as an `external mem` entry with its offset and array stride, so PeakRDL sees the whole map.

So there is no hole today. Three reasons to add a check of our own anyway:

*   **It is not ours and it is not documented.** Nobody would learn from any document that overlaps are refused; it was discovered by trying. If PeakRDL changed that behaviour, or a future topology passed through neither tool, the hole would open silently.
*   **It arrives late.** Configuration validation happens at phase 2; the overlap surfaces at 5 or 6, after the dependencies have been fetched and RTL emitted. The windows are all in hand when the map is built, so the check is roughly twenty lines and would move an existing error upstream.
*   **The list feature multiplies the occasions.** With `base_addr` accepting a list (see the heterogeneous-tiles entry) a user can overlap WITHIN one component, not only between components. That the two external tools would still catch it is likely but unverified.

**Do not ship it as a warning first** (user decision, 2026-08-26). A check that reports ten warnings of which one is real is not a check: it teaches the reader to ignore it. This one must be precise when it lands - one error, on a real overlap - which means the semantic work comes FIRST: deciding which shared addresses are deliberate (the `l2_isle` dual view of 3.8, the CIE window containing external memories, the host's internal scratchpad inside the host window) and which are defects (the L2 inside `LlcOutRegion` that fired the one-hot assertion in the sixth wave). Until that model exists, FlooGen and PeakRDL are the guards, and they are adequate.

Scope it honestly when it is written: this does not close a gap in coverage, it makes the coverage ours, uniform across families, earlier, and documented.

### 5.1 Crossbar-Family Coverage: What Remains After crux

The wall fell on 2026-08-06: `crux` builds hierarchically (host + preload target + typed isles in the top, the three async isles as child libraries) and runs to UART + EOT byte-identical to QuestaSim in 53 minutes and 207 MB. The campaign's repairs are all structural — two flow gaps fixed in the template (`--relative-includes`, component defines reaching the Verilator flags), the packed-ification and literal-completion patches on pulp_cluster/opentitan, the hci self-assignment fixes, the scm latch target, the VHDL root stubs — so nothing crux-specific remains in the flow. Open items:

*   **`crossbar_isle` is unprobed**: the macro build of the same family shares crux's graph, so it should fall cheaply — but "should" is what every layer of the onion said. A probe is the first step of any coverage claim.
*   **Fallback if the remaining walls resist**: stub pulp_cluster and the secure subsystem in the Verilator flow only, with the exact-signature stub generator fast-check already uses; hello world never exercises them, so the run still boots and prints UART. The coverage loss would have to be stated in the user documentation.
*   **Admitting the typed isles as hier blocks - IMPLEMENTED, validated on crossbar_isle 2026-08-15** (full suite pending as the commit gate). Both simulators run the complete offload on the de-typed tree, and the crossbar family's first end-to-end hierarchical Verilator run passes on 8 child libraries where 3 were admitted before. Two implementation notes beyond the agreed design: the header surgery is done on pyslang's syntax tree after the regex route produced three distinct defects in one evening (the method's docstring keeps the list), and arch_optimizer's four host-injected reg types bypass supported_params, so the IR builders drop custom-parameter overrides whose names the de-typing removed (comp_info['detyped_params']). One measurement for the record: crux_isle under Questa in suite conditions is byte-equivalent in behaviour, and the snitch-icache arbiter assertion that a manual ASSERTIONS=1 run surfaces at 2.3 us is pre-existing third-party reset noise (see section 3.3), not a regression of this work. Original design record follows. The route: stop emitting `parameter type` in the staged isle copies. For every staged isle the generator emits a small `{module_prefix}_{isle}_types_pkg` package (module_prefix derives from the TOP name, never project.name - the realignment rule, so nested macros stay unique) containing one `typedef <soc_pkg-qualified type> <parameter name>;` per type parameter it fills today, injects `import <that pkg>::*;` into the staged copy's header and deletes the `parameter type` lines - port declarations keep their names untouched, now resolved by the import - and stops passing the overrides at the instantiation site. Implementation notes discovered on the first survey: (1) the fill logic is a ~200-line role-based dispatch inside `build_crossbar_ir` (rtl_ir_builder.py:84-175); it must be EXTRACTED into a pure helper `(param, comp, soc_config, pkg) -> value` so the staging pass - which runs before the IR is built - can call the same function; (2) the staged copy is per ISLE TYPE while the fill is per COMPONENT ROLE (host / llc_port / plain slave), so the transform may only fire when every component of that type in the project agrees on the fill - otherwise keep the parameters and report the isle as not hier-eligible, loudly; (3) the NoC family needs the same treatment one level up (the universal tile's declared-type pass-through); crossbar first, since task 'extend hier blocks inside nested macros' consumes it there. Validation: Questa results must stay functionally identical (the resolved types are the same), the whole suite is the gate, and the crossbar's hier-block count should rise from 3 to ~9 minus the marked hyperbus.
*   **The pre-existing note on the same limitation**: a wrapper whose header carries `parameter type` cannot be a `hier_block` (Verilator's hier-parameters wrapper cannot serialize a type), which today keeps every sync isle in the top unit. That the excluded isles are the light ones is a tendency of crux, not an invariant — a heavy sync component (the L2 with its llc port is one already) lands in the top lane. The natural remedy is a generated flat shell per sync isle: the injected AXI/reg types are packed structs, so a shell with `logic [$bits(t)-1:0]` ports and pure conversion assigns — the macro boundary's published-AXI philosophy, one level down — becomes the hier block while the typed isle lives inside it. Serializing type parameters by name in the hier wrapper is the upstream-side alternative, worth a feature request but not a plan. the flow auto-stubs the roots of the graph's VHDL subtrees (`src/tools/gen_vhdl_stubs.py`) because Verilator has no VHDL front-end — functional coverage of those blocks belongs to QuestaSim's mixed-language runs. The known route to real license-free coverage is pre-translating the VHDL to a structural Verilog netlist with GHDL's yosys plugin (`ghdl-yosys-plugin`), then validating that the netlist simulates equivalently. Worth it only when a test actually drives the CAN. GHDL would also bring pyGHDL (libghdl), the mature replacement for the stub generator's deliberately entity-header-scoped regex parsing — one adoption, both upgrades.
*   **A crossbar build would be monolithic today**: `generate_verilator_config` emits hierarchical blocks only for NoC grids, so `cfg/crux.vlt` is empty — fine for probing, but the mesh measurements (~45 GB monolithic) say the isles should become `hier_block`s before a full crux build is attempted.
*   **The super examples stay out of scope regardless**: their unified Bender graphs carry the crossbar IPs (and their size compounds it) — though the measured hierarchical footprint (~3 GB per child against ~45 GB monolithic) may eventually relax the memory ceiling that limits `super_noc` today.

### 5.2 Performance Levers Not Yet Adopted

**Priority rule for the near-term work on this flow (set 2026-08-19): the primary objective is REDUCING TIME.** An item that does not shorten a build or a run goes below the ones that do, however elegant. Two exceptions, and both are argued on time: the hierarchy assertion, because a block that silently returns inlined gives back the hours an optimization won and costs more to diagnose (three such defects cost two campaigns in two days), and `--autoflush`, which costs nothing and saves human time reading a live transcript. Everything diagnostic - `--x-initial unique` in particular - is a separate quality initiative, not part of this push.

Ranked by expected payoff, to be re-ordered as the measurements land: (1) **warm builds with correct invalidation** - the largest lever, and the strategy the evidence points to: if a cold build's cost is neither the host (moving Cheshire out of the top barely changed it), nor the NoC routers (they live inside the tiles, as in MAGIA), nor hyperbus (it cannot leave the top - behavioural timing, section 5.3 - so retiring the type-parameter rule would not free it), then the winning move is not making a cold build cheaper but not paying for it repeatedly; (2) **the specialization collapse** of section 5.2.1, which removes real work from every build; (3) run-time flags, one variable at a time; (4) emptying the top unit, which after the two corrections above reduces to the preload memories alone (architected loading).

**Removed from the plan on 2026-08-20, kept here as catalogue hygiene only: pruning files that are never instantiated.** The measurement in section 5.2.-1 settles it - parsing all 3406 files of `noc` costs ELEVEN SECONDS, so the file count is not where the time goes and pruning would buy about ten. It stays worth doing when a dependency is touched for other reasons (the flist carries `top_earlgrey`, `ibex_lockstep` and `otp_ctrl` verification sequences that no shipped SoC instantiates), in the manifests where this repository's own rule puts such fixes, and it never means stubbing or dropping a block we do simulate - that would buy time with coverage, a trade this project does not make. But it is not an optimization, and it is not scheduled.

All deferred pending the flow's first real use; each needs its own measurement before entering the recipe.

*   **Explicit input stamps instead of the unconditional wipe** (the model is MAGIA's `verilator/verilator.mk`, read 2026-08-19). It tracks the Bender file list, the hier-block control file and the trace mode with separate stamps, and purges only what the changed input invalidates - including a targeted object purge when the trace mode changes, to avoid an ABI mismatch. That is the "reliable invalidation" this repository made a precondition for re-enabling between-build reuse when it disabled it wholesale (see the note above and section 5.3.0). Adopting it would turn our cold-only builds back into minutes-long warm ones, which is most of the gap between our numbers and theirs on repeat runs.
*   **The tuned flag set from MAGIA's `fc/verilator-squash` branch** (read 2026-08-19; its `fc/verilator` sibling is the untuned one, with `THREADS=1`, `-O2`, `JOBS=4`). The squash branch runs `-O3 --x-assign fast --x-initial fast --no-assert --timing --autoflush --threads 4`, with `VERILATOR_CFLAGS = -march=native` and `VERILATOR_JOBS = 16`. Checked against the official option reference before drawing conclusions: `--x-assign` and `--x-initial` both DEFAULT to `fast`, so MAGIA passing them explicitly changes nothing and they are not a difference between the two flows - we already run that way. What genuinely differs is `-O3` (Verilator's own optimization level), `--no-assert` (removes SVA at tool level, where we only pass `+define+ASSERTS_OFF`), `--autoflush`, `--threads 4`, `-march=native` and `JOBS=16`. Each is a one-variable experiment on `noc`, in this order of risk: `--autoflush` and `-O3` (harmless), `--no-assert` (loses the assertions the QuestaSim leg still checks), `--threads 4` (their own untuned branch warns of segfaults above 1, and our model carries DPI plus `--timing`), `-march=native` (ties the binary to the build host's CPU - never in an artifact shared across machines).

*   **`--x-initial unique` as a DIAGNOSTIC leg, the opposite direction from speed.** Verilator is two-state at run time, but it still has to substitute a value for the X the RTL contains (explicit `'x`, uninitialized variables and memories). With the default `fast` it picks whatever is cheapest, so a flop nobody resets holds a stable, usually convenient value and the bug is invisible; with `unique` the substitute is randomized per site and per instance, and a design that depends on X becomes nondeterministic across runs - which is how a two-state simulator recovers part of the X-pessimism a four-state one gives for free. The QuestaSim leg catches those bugs today through real X propagation; the Verilator leg silently does not. Worth one campaign leg on `noc`, not as a default.
*   **Flow robustness guards worth adopting regardless of speed** (also from MAGIA): (a) a post-build assertion that the hierarchy actually happened - every declared `hier_block` produced its library, the specialization count is the expected one, and the parent did NOT inline the module (their `check_hierarchy.py` looks for `module__params` specializations in `V<top>_classes.mk`). This is precisely the class of silent degradation that cost this project two days, so it belongs in `build-sim-verilator` as a failing check rather than in a reviewer's eye. (b) `--autoflush`, so a run's output reaches the transcript as it happens: block-buffered stdout repeatedly made a healthy simulation look stalled during the 2026-08-17/19 debugging.

*   **`--threads`**: multithreaded simulation runtime is the largest untapped wall-clock lever, but on the mesh it inflated *verilation* from minutes to hours per unit. Unusable in the edit-build-run loop; potentially right for production builds where one build serves many regression runs. Re-characterize on 5.050+ in isolation.
*   **UART clock independence**: the fast-UART knob landed on 2026-08-07 (`software_stack.test_app.baudrate`, firmware and testbench monitor sharing the one 16550 divisor the generator resolves — behaviour documented in `soc_configuration_guide.md`, section 5; the shipped examples run at divisor 3, ~11× less simulated time). Two refinements remain open. The generator still assumes a 100 MHz peripheral clock (`uart_freq` in `rtl_generator.py`): a project simulating at another frequency would get a silently scaled line rate. The mature pattern is astral's, where the firmware reads the frequency from the RTC register at runtime and derives the divisor itself — worth adopting when the clock tree starts varying between projects. And an auto-baud monitor (measure the start bit) would decouple the testbench from the divisor entirely, useful the day a test reprograms the UART mid-run.
*   **Leave unused components gated**: promoted from lever to specification on 2026-08-17 — see section 5.4.
*   **Re-admit the L2 tile into the `.vlt`**: the generated wrapper's parameter defaults read package struct constants (`AxiCfgJoin.*`), which the 5.050 hierarchical wrapper cannot rebuild, so the emission excludes it and the top unit keeps the heaviest memory tiles. **The obvious fix does not work**: demoting those parameters to body localparams in `universal_tile.sv.mako` (they are never driven from the top) was tried on 2026-08-06 and reverted — with the demotion in place, *generation itself* turns pathological on the mesh (the generator spins at 100% CPU with RSS climbing past 8 GB, and a fast-check on the resulting tree past 44 GB, against 52 s for the same run without it). The mechanism was not identified, only bisected: same tree, same commands, the demotion is the single differing variable (the obvious suspect, the stub generator's `neutralize_body_relative_param_defaults` regex, was measured at 0.00 s on every generated tile and is not it). The lead worth following first: the demotion also moves the `Preload*` localparams out of the header, and those are *read back* from the header by `get_isle_info` to build the testbench (`PreloadType`, `PreloadTemplate`, `PreloadNumGroups`, `PreloadBanksPerGroup`) — a downstream consumer losing a count it loops over is the shape of defect that produces exactly this signature. Whoever picks this up should find the *why* first: a pathology that large is a defect in its own right, and it may well bite another change that touches generated headers.

    **The plan, settled 2026-08-06.** Leave the geometry parameters in the tile header with plausible literal defaults, and have the top drive all of them at the instantiation. The struct read then happens in the parent, where it is evaluated to an integer before reaching the child, and the child's header is all literals — exactly what the hierarchical wrapper needs. It also leaves the `Preload*` localparams in the header, where the testbench generator reads them back, so it avoids the suspected cause of the pathology above. Three points decide whether it is done right:

    *   **Do not extract FlooGen's `OutIdWidth` formula.** It is a derived value, not a documented contract (4→2 and 3→1 on the mesh, a function of topology and endpoints), and replicating it in Python would couple us to logic that can change upstream without notice — visible only as truncated IDs, late. It is not needed: the top emits the *expression* (`<noc_pkg>::AxiCfgN.OutIdWidth`), which its own scope resolves. The same argument rules out reordering the phases to read the FlooGen package (tiles render in phase 3, FlooGen runs in phase 5) — nothing is gained for the cost.
    *   **Literal defaults are a hazard to mitigate.** Today the default *is* the correct value, so a forgotten override is harmless; with literals, a forgotten override silently mis-sizes the tile — the CDC misalignment class made fatal in `wiring.py` on 2026-08-05, which is also the backstop that would catch it. Use the values the generator already resolves (`resolve_noc_id_widths`, `resolve_noc_user_widths`) rather than arbitrary numbers, so the failure stays improbable as well as loud.
    *   **`AxiCfgJoin` stays inside the tile**, since the join types and the Join adapter's own configuration derive from it. Rebuild it there *from the driven parameters* so the top remains the single source; mind that the tile's `AxiCfgJoin.InIdWidth` is the `max` while `AxiInIdWidth` is that `max + 1`, so one field is derived rather than copied.

    **Probed on 2026-08-06, and the answer moves the goalposts.** A struct-member read as an instantiation override IS accepted — Verilator passed it without complaint. But with the geometry defaults made literal, the L2 tile then failed one level deeper, with an internal error on Verilator's own generated file: `__hierParameters.v:2: V3LinkDot.cpp:496: Module/etc never assigned a symbol entry?`, on the leading `typedef struct packed {`. Serializing the child's parameterization emits its boundary AXI types as typedefs with nested anonymous packed structs, which Verilator cannot then consume. So the plan above is necessary but **not sufficient**: what actually keeps this tile out of the `.vlt` is the struct types at its boundary, not the parameter defaults. The enabler is the flat generated shell described in section 5.1 — plain-vector ports, types rebuilt inside — which removes types from the boundary altogether and covers the crossbar's sync isles in the same move. Do that first; the literal defaults are then a small tidy-up rather than a prerequisite.
*   **The super_noc top's build cost, measured while validating the macro descent (2026-08-15/16)**: even with both nested macros descended (15 declared blocks, 54 child libraries built clean in 13 minutes), the residual top - three Cheshire hosts, four preload L2 tiles, two macro wrappers - emits **1010 MB of C++ headers**, and that number governs everything downstream: the precompiled-header generation alone ran 4.5 h without finishing (both .gch at 99% CPU; they gate all remaining objects, so 24 cores sat behind two compiles), and a textual-PCH fallback is non-viable (every TU re-parses the gigabyte: 24 objects in 12 hours). The build was abandoned unfinished by decision (2026-08-16): the pathology PREDATES the descent - yesterday's 12 h baseline contained the same top plus the macros - and its cures are already queued: section 2.1 removes the three Cheshires (the header mass), the preload hook removes the L2 tiles, and `--output-split` / per-top `-O1` / PCH policy belong to 5.2's lever list. Two flow defects found on the way, both reproduced twice: the hierarchical build emitted corrupt C++ under `-j 48` a SECOND time (same truncation signature, mesh-family cluster tile again; the first retry at `-j 24` re-verilated it clean, upgrading the -j suspicion from wip 5.1), and `make generate` deletes `generated/vhdl_stubs/`, whose timestamps then cascade a full re-verilation of every child library on the next hierarchical make - the recipe should depend on `prep-sim-verilator` instead of assuming the stubs.
*   **Free the preload targets from the top unit**: a tile named in `testbench.preload_memories` must stay out of the `.vlt` because the testbench's `$readmemh` reaches into it with a dotted path, which cannot cross a hier-block boundary. The subtleties are that hier_block is per MODULE while preload is per INSTANCE (`noc_isle` preloads one of its eight L2 tiles), and that the generated RTL must stay a function of the hardware description alone — a simulation setting may never change the sources (name-specializing the boot instance and per-instance `PreloadEn` parameters both fail this bar and were set aside). The clean shape is a runtime-keyed hook, identical in every instance, under `` `ifndef SYNTHESIS ``: an `initial` that asks `$value$plusargs` for a key derived from `%m` and `$readmemh`s only if the testbench passed that instance's path — per-instance selection moves entirely to run arguments, like `+fast_boot`, and the wrapper's own Preload* localparams handle the interleaved-bank splitting better than the testbench can. To verify by probe before trusting it: `%m` semantics across a hier-block boundary, and dynamic plusarg format strings under the license-free tool. Fallback is today's behavior — preload targets stay in the top: a build-lane cost, never a correctness one. Worth little on `noc` (the target is the small narrow scratchpad), much on `noc_isle` / `super_noc`, whose preload target is a gated L2 tile.
*   **Make the build parallelism configurable from the environment**: the generated `sim/sim.mk` hardcodes `VERILATOR_JOBS ?= 48`, used both for the hierarchical verilation and for the C++ compile of the top unit — the phase that briefly takes ~50 cores of a shared machine. The `?=` makes it overridable on the command line, which is enough for a one-off but not for a project or a site: there is no way to set it in `*_env.yml`, the test suite does not pass it through, and the default ignores how many cores the host actually has. It should come from the environment configuration like every other path and tool setting, with a sensible default derived from the machine rather than a constant. Same question applies to the QuestaSim leg, which today runs its compile single-threaded.
*   **Cross-project hierarchical-library reuse**: today every project re-verilates everything (own Mdir, own flist). Donor-derived module names are stable across projects by the naming rule, so ccache already absorbs identical C++; a deliberate shared block-library cache (keyed on module, sources, flags and Verilator version) could remove re-verilation too. Second-generation work, worth designing when the supers enter the flow's scope.

**Between-build reuse of the hierarchical Verilator tree (disabled 2026-08-18).** `build-sim-verilator` now wipes `verilator_work/` at the start of every build (skippable with `VERILATOR_KEEP_WORK=1` for targeted debug). Verilator's hierarchical cache proved unsafe twice within one day: it validates neither its own outputs (a build aborted mid-write leaves truncated generated headers the next run fails on in C++ compilation) nor the children's sources (a child whose RTL changed is silently relinked stale - the l2_isle instrumentation of 2026-08-18 ran against a child built nine hours earlier). The measured benefit was small (1h25 warm vs 1h44 cold on comparable SoCs: the top re-verilates on any change anyway, and ccache absorbs the C++ of unchanged children), so the lever stays off until a reliable invalidation exists - either upstream (children tracking their source hashes) or ours (hash the per-child source set at generation time and wipe on mismatch). Re-enabling without that is how silent stale-child results come back.

### 5.2.-2 The Hierarchical Libraries Were Built and Not Used: Root Cause and Fix (2026-08-20)

**Status: fixed, validated on `noc`, remaining work is the guard and the other six projects.**

**The symptom, before the fix.** On `noc`, of the five modules the `.vlt` declares as hierarchical blocks, only ONE - `mesh_dummy_tile` - was actually referenced by the top model. The other four are compiled into libraries that the parent never links against: the parent inlines those modules instead, so their logic is generated TWICE (3095 `act_comb` functions in the child library against 37941 in the top, roughly the child's content once per instance). No warning is emitted; Verilator simply treats them as ordinary modules.

**The root cause, found 2026-08-20: a missing option, not a property of our RTL.** Verilator substitutes a prebuilt hierarchical library for a block's instances only when `--hierarchical-params-file` is passed alongside `--hierarchical-block`; without it the parent silently de-parameterizes the block and inlines one clone per instance. The engine emits a parameters file of its own only for blocks carrying a `parameter type`, so a design with none - ours - never gets the option and never boxes anything that declares a parameter at all. `mesh_dummy_tile` boxed because it declares none, and its wrapper therefore keeps the original module name; every parameterized block gets a mangled name that nothing rewrites the instantiations to.

The option only needs to name a PARSEABLE file: parameter declarations are not required, and the file Verilator would have written is itself empty. Ollivander now generates it next to the `.vlt` (`<top>_hier_params.v`) and the flow passes it. Proven on a minimal eight-module testcase covering every parameter shape we use - plain integral, 64-bit, package-scoped enum, struct-typed, struct-field default, `parameter type`: with the option absent only the parameter-free module boxes, with it present all eight box and the parent emits none of their logic. Credit where due: the mechanism is documented in MAGIA's `target/verilator/src/magia_hier_params.v`, down to the Verilator source lines that gate it (`V3Param.cpp` `m_hierSubRun`, `V3HierBlock.cpp`).

**What this invalidates.** Every conclusion drawn from build-time comparisons before this date was measuring a FLAT build with extra steps: crux (host boxed) against crux_isle (host inlined) costing the same, "partitioning is not a time lever", and the earlier work that raised the block count from 3 to 7 (tasks #23, #24) producing no measurable gain. The mechanics of those changes are sound; their benefit was never collected.

**What the fix is NOT.** Baking the resolved parameter values into the generated module as defaults, and emitting the instantiation without overrides, was the candidate remedy while the parameters themselves were believed to be the disqualifier. It is unnecessary: with the option in place a block boxes whatever its parameters look like. The same goes for the "shell" - an extra generated hierarchy level, parameter-free, wrapping tile and isle alike - which was designed for this problem and is not needed for it. The identity-to-port change that preceded all this keeps its own justification (one module instead of one per instance, section 5.2.1) and is unaffected.

**The acceptance criterion changes with it**: not "the library was produced" - it always was - but **"the top model references the mangled wrapper"**. That check belongs in the build as a failing assertion, otherwise the defect returns silently the first time someone adds a per-instance parameter.

**The `pulp_cluster_isle` exception is withdrawn.** It was accepted earlier the same day on the reading that a value needed at elaboration time (its window base reaches the IP through `Cfg.ClusterBaseAddr` and from there into four child parameter overrides) forbade boxing inside a nested macro. Parameters are not the obstacle, so there is nothing to except and no need to make the macro non-relocatable to work around it.

**Still open: the residual per-instance parameterization.** Verilator builds one child library per distinct parameterization, and that is unchanged - it is what section 5.2.1 addresses. Boxing now takes effect for each of those libraries instead of none of them.

**The option is documented as "internal use", and it has one measured trap - which our contracts already exclude.** An empty file is safe unless a declared block carries a `parameter type` overridden per instance. Measured on that exact case: the engine writes a real parameters file per specialization (each carrying its `typedef`), our command-line file arrives LAST in the child's arguments, and both instances bind to a single specialization - the 16-bit instance simulated by the 32-bit library, the other built and orphaned. Value parameters are unaffected, also measured: two instances differing by a value produce two libraries and the top references both.

**Why the exposure is structural, not incidental.** What gets boxed is always the generated tile/isle wrapper, and the standardization contracts keep that boundary type-free: the underlying isles declare up to fourteen type parameters each (`spatz_cluster_isle`), injected one level inside the wrapper per contract section 2.4, with the types reaching it through the per-isle packages of task #23. Every declared block across the seven examples resolves to `parameter type = 0`, promoted donor blocks included. Reintroducing the risk would take putting a type parameter on a WRAPPER boundary, which is what the packages mechanism exists to avoid.

**The guard this still calls for, with the rest of the build mechanics: every child library the build produces must be referenced by the top model.** One check catches both failure modes - the missing-option case, where nothing is referenced, and the type-parameter case, where a library is built and orphaned. Its first justification is the failure that actually happened here: an internal option, silently ineffective for months, that no warning reported. Being internal, it may also change across releases without notice.

### 5.2.-1 Where a Cold Verilator Build Actually Spends Its Time (measured on `noc`, 2026-08-20)

Every optimization below is ranked against this decomposition, obtained on the `noc` example (3406 files in the Verilator file list, 27 child specializations for 5 declared block types):

| Step | Time | Share of a cold build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preprocessing only (`verilator -E`) | 2 s | ~0% |
| Parsing every file (`--lint-only` with a trivial `--top-module`) | 11 s | ~0% |
| Full front end (`--lint-only` on the real top): parse + ELABORATE | 60 min | 51% |
| C++ generation, compilation and link (build total minus the above) | ~58 min | 49% |
| **Cold build** | **118 min** | |
| Repeat build, ccache "warm" | 122 min | ccache hit rate 4.19%, 5 GB cap - useless |
| Repeat build after the fix, 50 GB cap | 5m1s against 6m58s | ccache hit rate **98.48%**, zero cleanups |
| Warm build (`VERILATOR_KEEP_WORK=1`, nothing changed) | **6 s** | |
| Simulation run | 24 m | QuestaSim on the same design: 9m31s |

**Every figure above was measured while nothing was boxed** (5.2.-2): the front end was elaborating the whole design once for the child libraries and again for the top, which is what the 60 minutes were. With `--hierarchical-params-file` in place the same cold build on `noc` takes **7m25s** and the run **19m37s** (against 24m16s), UART and EOT unchanged - a full cycle down from ~142 minutes to ~27.

**ccache was not useless, it was suffocating.** The 4.19% hit rate came with 2471 cleanups against a 5 GB cap, while one project's objects alone reach 2.2 GB (`noc`) and 5.7 GB (`crux`, of which 4.9 GB is the child libraries): every project evicted the previous one, so reuse across a campaign was structurally impossible. Raised to 50 GB on 2026-08-20, a repeat build of `noc` hits **98.48%** with zero cleanups and drops from 6m58s to 5m1s - the saving bounded, as expected, by the C++ half alone, since the front end is not cacheable. The cumulative figure is the one that matters: on `crux` the C++ half is 12m51s of a 25m9s build, so the same hit rate is worth twelve minutes on a single project, and a campaign repeated over what a change did NOT touch recovers the sum of seven such halves. What does not benefit: a change that alters every project's generated C++, where no cap helps.

**Boxing a block makes the SIMULATION faster too, not only the build - three measurements agree (2026-08-20).** `noc`, which inlines one memory tile, runs at 3.523 us/s against `noc_isle`'s 2.812 with eight inlined; `crux`, whose host isle is boxed, runs at 2.444 us/s against `crux_isle`'s 1.520 with the same host inlined because `boot_mode: force` makes the testbench reach into it. That is the opposite of what the DPI call overhead would suggest, and the likely reason is the same one that makes `-Os` beat `-O2` here: a child library is an independent model with its own eval function and working set, while inlining a large block into the top produces one huge function with worse code locality. None of the three pairs is a single-knob experiment - they are different projects - but `crux` against `crux_isle` is the cleanest, since the dominant difference is one block moving across the boundary. Two consequences: the old "partitioning is not a time lever" verdict was wrong in BOTH dimensions, not just at build time; and every block that stops being reached by the testbench (chapter 2) is worth more than the build-time argument alone suggests.

**A nested macro's child libraries are NOT reusable across projects, and the reason is worth its own investigation (found 2026-08-20).** `super_crossbar` nests the Mesh macro from `noc_isle`, and both builds produce a child library with the SAME mangled name - `Vmesh_isle_compute_clusters_tile_6`, so the parameter set matches - yet the generated C++ differs in twelve lines, so ccache cannot serve the parent from the donor's objects (measured: the hit rate fell from 21.58% to 17.28% across that build while the cache grew by 4.5 GB of fresh objects). Three of the differences are cosmetic - an absolute source path embedded in the emitted code, and a hash-named temporary that follows from it - and `ccache`'s `base_dir` would absorb those. The fourth is not: an iDMA translation unit emits `VL_TIME_UNITED_Q(1000)` in the donor and `VL_TIME_UNITED_Q(1)` in the parent, a thousand-fold difference in time-unit conversion for the same module with the same parameters. **The likely cause is `` `timescale `` leakage through compilation order**: the directive persists across files, 6 of `noc_isle`'s 1198 sources and 9 of `super_crossbar`'s 2191 declare their own, and the two file lists differ in content and order, so a source without a directive inherits a different one in each project. That is a hypothesis consistent with the evidence, not an isolated proof - confirming it means finding which declaring file precedes the iDMA sources in each list. It matters beyond caching: the same IP compiled at a different time resolution depending on which project pulls it in is the shape of a defect that reads as "works in one project, not in the other".

**What this leaves.** Pruning the file list was never a lever and still is not (parsing all 3406 files costs eleven seconds). Partitioning looked like a non-lever because the comparison behind it - crux at 2h19 with its host boxed against crux_isle at 2h20 with it inlined - was measuring two flat builds; that verdict is void and the whole hierarchical mechanism is now worth what it always promised. Two conclusions survive intact: warm builds are still the cheapest possible outcome (6 s), and the C++ half is still paid in full by a ccache that hits 4.19%. The specialization collapse of 5.2.1 keeps its rationale, since one library per parameterization is unchanged - only now each one is actually used.

**`--threads` measured on 2026-08-20: it segfaults, and the trigger is our own `-O2`.** With `--threads 4` or `8` the model dies after four seconds, before a single UART line, inside `eval_initial__TOP__Vtiming__1__0 [clone .actor]` - a C++20 coroutine frame of a top-level timed `initial` - on the MAIN thread, with every worker still parked in `pthread_cond_wait`. Rebuilt at `-O0` the same model runs past two minutes, so the fault is optimization-dependent, which points at the coroutine lowering rather than at the testbench's logic. The comparison that localizes it: MAGIA runs `--threads 4` WITH `--timing` on this very host (140 us simulated in 60 s, no crash) and passes no `OPT_FAST`, taking verilated.mk's default; we force `-O2`. So the open question was a trade to measure - `-O2` single-threaded against `-Os` multi-threaded - not an incompatibility. **Measured the same day: `--threads 4` with the top at `-Os` runs in 6m33s against 19m37s, UART and EOT unchanged** - three times faster, bringing `noc`'s whole cycle to 14m11s. There is no top-versus-children split, and an earlier reading of these logs that claimed one was wrong: `-CFLAGS` lands on every compile line, parent and child alike, and `verilated.mk` deliberately places `OPT_FAST` BEFORE the other flags (line 287), so a later `-Os` wins everywhere. The configuration measured at 6m33s is therefore `-Os` everywhere - which is exactly `verilated.mk`'s default, i.e. what MAGIA gets by never overriding it. Their own history points the same way: commit `eacc9d7` on `fc/verilator-squash` REPLACES `-CFLAGS -O2` with `-CFLAGS -march=native`, dropping forced C++ optimization rather than raising it. Two things must ship with it: the coupling documented for whoever tries `--threads` on a generated SoC (unaided, they get a four-second segfault with no clue), and an upstream report - the second after the V3ProtectLib one. Two facts bound the upside: peak memory was 3.5 GB at both 4 and 8 threads, so the supers are not excluded on that account; and since the workers never received work before the crash, `--threads` alone can only parallelize the top's stitching logic, which is why the workers-on-boxed-blocks lever below matters more.

**FIXED with the `simulation:` section (2026-08-21, pending the section's gate): the pair now switches together at make level.** The generated Makefile derives `VERILATOR_THREAD_OPTS` from `VERILATOR_THREADS` through a conditional - values above 1 emit `--threads N -CFLAGS -DVL_TIME_CONTEXT`, 0 and 1 emit neither - so the knob is safe from the YAML (`simulation.verilator.threads`) and from the command line alike; verified in all five states (default, YAML 1, CLI 1, CLI 0, CLI 8). The paragraphs below record the defect as found, for the history of why the conditional exists.

**ATTRIBUTION OF EVERY t=0 SEGFAULT IN THIS CHAPTER: RESOLVED AS ccache POISONING (2026-08-22).** All of these crash observations - the `-O2` coroutine segfault above, the 2x2 threads/define matrix below, the super_crux 8-thread crash further down - were produced through ccache 3.7.7 on Verilator's precompiled-header builds, a combination proven to poison objects (stale class layouts served across "from-scratch" rebuilds, since work-dir wipes never touch the cache). Two independent disproofs closed the case: the first full gate of 2026-08-21 ran crux with `-DVL_TIME_CONTEXT` present and NO `--threads` - the exact quadrant the 2x2 calls a segfault - and passed; and the decisive rerun of 2026-08-22 rebuilt the "scheduler-crashing" hardened testbench from scratch with `CCACHE_DISABLE=1` and ran the full offload to EOT. The SPEED measurements in this chapter stand (poison crashes, it does not slow); every CRASH narrative below is retained as history but its causal claim is withdrawn - no upstream report will be filed from any of them (the super_crux 8-thread rerun stays queued as a low-priority confirmation). The make-level coupling of the threads/define pair stays as sound practice; the operational lesson lives in the flow mitigations (suite cache-free, pch/ccache split in sim.mk) and in `upstream_pr_candidates.md`'s resolved row.

**A defect in what commit 4eba523 shipped, found the same night: the configuration only works WITH threads, and `VERILATOR_THREADS` cannot be turned down.** Measured on `crux`, everything else identical and `-DVL_TIME_CONTEXT` present throughout: `--threads 4` passes the gate (8m39s), `--threads 1` builds and then segfaults after four seconds with no UART, and **omitting `--threads` entirely segfaults too** (12m56s build, then six seconds). So this is not "1 is a broken value": the non-threaded model is what no longer runs, and the threaded one is what works - the reverse of the morning, when the single-threaded model ran and the threaded one crashed.

**Confirmed by the fourth measurement: the two flags are coupled, and neither half is valid alone.** With no `--threads` AND no forced define - so `--main` gives it to the top only, the asymmetry of before the fix - `crux` runs in **7m35s and passes** (UART and EOT present). The full 2x2, all on `crux`:

| | define on the top only | define forced on parent and children |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **no `--threads`** | **passes, 7m35s** | segfault |
| **`--threads 4`** | segfault | **passes, 8m39s** |

So the fix is not to make `--threads` conditional on its own: the flag and the define must be switched **together**, because the children need the legacy time path exactly when the model is not threaded and the context path exactly when it is.

**And this finally supplies the control measurement the family-dependence question lacked.** On `crux`, four threads cost **8m39s against 7m35s** - about 14% SLOWER than no threads at all, everything else equal. The suspicion raised by the gate's tool comparison is therefore confirmed with a proper baseline rather than inferred: on the crossbar family the shipped default is a small net loss, while on `noc` it was a 3x gain. A per-topology default, or a documented per-project override, is the decision this leaves open.

The reasoning that led there, kept because it explains the mechanism: the two flags are **coupled**. Before the fix, `--main` gave the top `-DVL_TIME_CONTEXT` and the boxed children never got it; that asymmetry was survivable single-threaded and fatal threaded. Forcing the define on both cured the threaded case, and appears to have broken the non-threaded one - which would mean the children need the legacy time path exactly when the model is not threaded. If dropping BOTH the flag and the define restores the run, the fix is not to make `--threads` conditional on its own but to switch the pair together, since neither half is valid without the other.

Two things are certain regardless of that test. The template interpolates `--threads $(VERILATOR_THREADS)` unconditionally, so **the knob has no off position** - an empty value leaves a dangling flag - and a user reaching for 1 to disable threading gets a segfault instead. And "one thread" is therefore NOT the control measurement for "four threads", so the family-dependence question below still has no baseline. Nothing that ships is broken: the default is 4 and that is the configuration the seven-project gate validated.

**Thread count above 4: measured on 2026-08-21, and reverted.** The supers tried `threads: 8` in a full gate plus a two-arm isolation. On `super_noc` eight threads bought nothing (22m20s against 21m44s at four) - that axis is final. On `super_crossbar` the run segfaulted at simulated time zero inside a top-level timing coroutine, with the coupled define correctly present; the isolation exonerated `compile_jobs` (jobs at 32 still crashed, threads at 4 with jobs at 64 passed, 14m39s). *The crash's attribution is CLOSED (2026-08-22): the `CCACHE_DISABLE=1` rerun at 8 threads built from scratch and ran the full offload to EOT - poisoning confirmed, upstream candidacy dead. The same rerun also completed the speed axis for super_crux: 18m13s at 8 threads against 14m39s at 4, i.e. slower, matching super_noc's no-gain result. The verdict is now final on both axes for both supers: stay at 4.* Regardless of the verdict: the examples stay at the default 4 (8 buys nothing where it runs), and `compile_jobs: 64` is adopted on both supers - measured -26/-27% on their cold builds, models validated end-to-end.

**Open, and deliberately left open for the commit that ships `--threads 4`: the gain may be family-dependent.** On `crux` the campaign of 2026-08-20 ran the simulation in 10m15s, against 9m03s measured the same morning single-threaded - i.e. threads appear to COST about 13% there, while on `mesh` they returned 3x. The explanation that fits is structural: `mesh` has sixteen identical compute tiles plus eight L2 tiles to spread, `crux` has eight isles that are mostly single instances, and since boxed blocks are evaluated inline on the calling thread (`hier_workers -workers 0`) there is nothing to spread - only synchronization to pay. The attribution is NOT established, because that comparison also moved `-O2` to `-Os`; the control measurement is `crux` with `VERILATOR_THREADS=1` at `-Os`, agreed to be run AFTER this commit rather than blocking it. If it confirms, a single default cannot serve both families and the choice becomes per-topology, or is left to `VERILATOR_EXTRA_FLAGS` with the reasoning documented.

**The build-time half of the `--threads` question is nearly meaningless**, and the measurement that suggested otherwise was wrong: the flag does not change the children's generated C++, so a threaded build reuses their objects from ccache (88-90% hit rate) and finishes FASTER than the reference. Only the run time is worth comparing, and only against a reference taken with the cache in the same state.

**It also reopened `--threads`, deliberately absent so far because it "inflated verilation from minutes to hours per unit".** That cost was paid on a top that elaborated the entire flattened design; on a 68-file top it is a different question, and a build that now costs seven minutes can absorb a multiple of that if the run halves. Two further knobs sit underneath, both visible in the wrappers Verilator generates: every boxed block is emitted with `profile_data -cost 64'd0` and consequently `hier_workers -workers 16'd0`, so sixteen identical tiles are evaluated in sequence on the calling thread. The `.vlt` is ours to generate, so both pragmas are reachable - the obstacle is that the DPI function names carry the specialization suffix, unknown before the build, so it needs a post-build pass or a collected profile. Measure one knob at a time, and watch memory: the front end alone reached 54 GB on `noc`.

### 5.2.0 Running the Suite's Projects in Parallel (noted, not planned)

`make test-all` walks its projects strictly in sequence, so the 2026-08-19 gate took twelve hours of which eight were three Verilator builds that never interact. On a host with this much memory and this many cores, two or three projects at a time would cut a campaign's wall clock more than any single-flow optimization.

**Deliberately not planned (decision 2026-08-20).** The benefit accrues to whoever DEVELOPS Ollivander and runs seven projects at once, not to the user who generates one SoC and simulates it; the queue is spent on what helps the latter. Recorded here so the idea is not rediscovered as new. If it is ever picked up, the constraints are: peak memory per build (a single Verilator front end reached 54 GB on `noc`), QuestaSim license count, and concurrent appends to `soc_cfg_examples/test_summary.log`, which today assumes one writer.

### 5.2.2 Three Build-Time Levers: Measured, Deliberately Not Planned (2026-08-20)

Recorded so the measurements survive; the decision of the same day is that the flow's performance is adequate and the effort goes to the four priorities listed at the top of this document. Numbers taken from `super_noc`'s twenty child libraries, separating EMISSION (the child's own verilation) from COMPILATION for the three heaviest blocks:

| Block | files | size | emission | compilation | final single-TU tail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `crux_isle_security_island_isle` | 510 | 1.4 GB | 17 s | 624 s | **196 s** |
| `crux_isle_spatz_cluster_isle` | 514 | 1.5 GB | 18 s | 369 s | 1 s |

### 5.2.1 Tile Uniformity: the Hierarchical Dividend We Are Paying For and Not Collecting

**The measurement (2026-08-19, noc_subtile).** The project's `.vlt` declares **4** hierarchical blocks; the build produced **28** child specializations. Verilator creates one child library per distinct PARAMETERIZATION, and the generated top passes per-instance parameters: two compute-cluster tiles differ by `InstanceBaseAddr` alone (`64'h21000000` vs `64'h21100000`), everything else identical. So sixteen identical clusters are verilated and compiled sixteen times, which is precisely the cost hierarchical verilation exists to avoid.

**Why MAGIA is fast and we are not** (the comparison that produced this finding): its mesh is homogeneous by construction, the flow REQUIRES it (`mesh_dv=1`) and a script enforces `VERILATOR_EXPECTED_TILE_SPECIALIZATIONS = 1`. One tile tree, verilated once, instantiated sixteen times. What is NOT established is how the two factors divide: they also run a tuned flag set we do not (section 5.2), so "one specialization instead of 28" is a measured structural difference, while its share of the wall-clock gap is still unmeasured. The two experiments are independent and both belong in the queue - collapse the specializations on `noc` and measure, then add the flags one at a time on the same design.

**What to do.** Make instance identity travel as PORTS instead of parameters wherever elaboration does not need it. `InstanceBaseAddr` and `InstanceWindowSize` are decoded at run time by address comparators, so a constant driven from the top synthesizes identically while collapsing sixteen specializations into one. Candidates, in order: the identity parameters introduced by the window convention (section 3.9), then any NoC coordinate or ID that the tile only forwards. Some parameters genuinely cannot move (array sizes, type widths); the goal is not zero but "one specialization per tile TYPE".

**Guard the invariant, do not hope for it** (MAGIA's `check_hierarchy.py` is the model): the build should assert the specialization count against the number of declared blocks and fail when a parameter creeps back in. Without it the collapse silently decays the first time someone adds a per-instance parameter.

**Expected size of the win**: on `noc_subtile` it is the difference between 28 and ~4 child verilations plus their C++ compilations - the dominant term of the build after the top unit. To be measured against the phase breakdown of `noc` (children emission / top verilation / C++ compilation), which is the next measurement queued.

**How to develop it, cheapest check first** (the point of the order is that each level costs an order of magnitude more than the previous one):

1. **`fast-check`** (~1 min): catches an elaboration mistake in the moved parameter immediately.
2. **QuestaSim simulation** (~12 min on `noc`): the FUNCTIONAL verdict, and the most sensitive one available for this particular change. A parameter that becomes a port can end up undriven or driven late; four-state semantics turn that into an X that propagates until an address comparison stops matching, and the boot fails visibly. Verilator would not show it - it is two-valued already, and the tuned flag set would deliberately erase X - so proving functionality under QuestaSim FIRST is a correctness argument, not only a speed one.
3. **Verilator phase 1 only** (~15-20 min): count the child directories. This is the metric the change exists for - one specialization per tile TYPE - and it needs no compilation at all, so the collapse can be iterated on without paying for a build.
4. **Full Verilator build** (~2-3 h): pay it once, when the count is already right, to measure the actual dividend.

The crossbar family gains nothing from the collapse (its isles are single-instance) but shares the isle sources, so `crux` runs as the non-regression sentinel at level 2 before any Verilator work.

### 5.3 Behavioural Timing Inside Hierarchical Blocks: an Explicit Mark

**The finding** (2026-08-13). Verilator refuses to build a hierarchically verilated block whose subtree contains any timing construct: `Unsupported: --lib-create with --timing and delays`. The message carries no source location, and on `super_noc` it landed after 35 minutes of verilation, on the nested Crux macro tile. The construct is a single line of a simulation-only model:

```systemverilog
// hyperbus/models/configurable_delay.behav.sv:36
always @(clk) clk_o <= #(real'(delay_i)*78ps + 10ps) clk;
```

Confirmed by construction: with that one assignment guarded, the same block verilates clean (22.1k log lines, 1471 files emitted, zero errors), and with it restored the refusal returns. It is an intra-assignment delay, which is why a search for statement-position `#` delays and for `assign #d` never found it. The model is not synthesizable and does not pretend to be: the IP ships it under `- target: test` alone, next to an FPGA variant that is a bare Xilinx `IBUF` ignoring `delay_i` outright - so no flow other than behavioural simulation models that phase shift at all.

**Why the crossbar family never saw it, and why that is an accident.** `generate_verilator_config` makes tiles the block candidates on a mesh and isles on a crossbar. The hyperbus isle is not among the crossbar's blocks, so its delay lands in the top unit where `--timing` is not only legal but required; on the mesh the nested macro sits inside a *tile*, and a tile block encloses all ten isles of the macro, hyperbus included. But hyperbus is outside the crossbar's block set only because of the `parameter type` exclusion - a workaround for a Verilator 5.050 internal error that section 5.1 proposes to REMOVE. The day it is removed, hyperbus becomes a block candidate and the refusal reaches `crux` and `super_crossbar`, which pass today by coincidence.

**The design** (agreed 2026-08-13). Make the reason explicit and independent of that coincidence, with a pragma in the component header, in the family of the `// BENDER:` and `// DEFINE:` pragmas `sv_parser.py` already extracts:

```systemverilog
// VERILATOR: exclude_hier_block="behavioural timing: hyperbus configurable_delay models a
//            32-tap delay line with an intra-assignment delay"
```

The rule in `generate_verilator_config` is two clauses, and the second is what makes the mark worth more than a special case: a marked component is never a `hier_block`, **and neither is any candidate whose subtree contains one**. The nested macro tile is then excluded *because it contains hyperbus*, not because it nests a macro - so the rule survives a change of granularity instead of needing a rewrite, and it keeps working if the block set later descends to the macro's own isles.

**Residual work, in order:**

*   **Propagate the mark across macro export.** A nested macro's isles are generated modules imported from the child project; the parent has to learn that one of them is marked. Ties into the macro-export ladder (section 3.9).
*   **Derive the list of components to mark instead of guessing it.** hyperbus is certain. The shapes now known to trip the refusal: statement-position and intra-assignment delays, and `fork ... join_none` inside a class declared in a package - the last one measured on an eight-line design, and it fires even when the class is never used, because classes in a package are not pruned the way an uninstantiated module is. Constructs measured harmless in the same setting: `fork ... join`, `join_any`, `wait`, `disable fork`, and a lone `semaphore` or `mailbox`.
*   **Descend the block set to the macro's own isles - implementation plan (2026-08-15, de-typing done and committed as its prerequisite).** Key realisation: `hier_block` is a per-MODULE declaration, depth-independent, so the parent need not restructure the excluded tile at all - it only has to add `hier_block` lines for the macro's inner isle modules, and Verilator carves them out of the inlined tile by name. Three steps: (1) the macro pragma's merged mark must name the staged MODULE (`crux_isle_hyperbus_isle`), not the child component (`hyperbus`), so the parent can exclude exactly the marked isle - the child generator knows the mapping at emission time; (2) the parent parses the mark's module names (tokens before ':' in the merged reason) and derives the macro's child prefix from them; (3) in generate_verilator_config, a tile excluded through a contained mark triggers the descent: every required file `<child_prefix>_*_isle.sv` whose module is unmarked and passes `_wrapper_params_ok` becomes a `hier_block` (the files resolve through the parent's require staging into the child's generated hw). The macro's internal l2 needs no preload exclusion in the parent - the parent testbench preloads only its own memories (the nested boot is section 3.9's third rung, untouched). Validation: super_noc build time against the measured 9 h front-end baseline, then the full suite. Originally recorded as an optimisation, promoted to planned work by the first measurement (2026-08-13/14): with the tile excluded, `super_noc`'s top unit carries the whole nested macro and **Verilator's front end alone took about nine hours** (~12 h for the full build; the resulting model is 766 MB and `verilator_work` 47 GB). The same RTL - Cheshire, spatz cluster, pulp cluster, security island - builds in **34 minutes end to end** in `super_crossbar`, where it sits in three isle-level child libraries. A factor of fifteen, on the same content, decided by partitioning alone. Declaring the macro's internal isles instead would keep the lanes for the heavy ones and leave hyperbus alone in the top, which is what the crossbar family already does. Two things to establish first: whether the imported `crux_isle_*` wrappers pass the existing parameter rule (in `super_crossbar` only three isles out of ten do), and how the mark's subtree clause behaves once a marked isle sits beside eligible siblings - it should keep exactly hyperbus out, which is the whole point of having phrased the rule causally.
*   **Report the guard-vs-mark decision if it is ever reopened.** Guarding the delay under `ifndef VERILATOR` builds too, but it removes the 90° phase shift the DDR capture path depends on, and it does so silently: acceptable only with a loud `$error` when `delay_i` is non-zero, and pointless once the mark exists.
*   **Re-examine the `hier-icache` registry patch, which may be unnecessary.** It was restored on 2026-08-13 after the entry had gone missing while its comment survived, and the comment's stated reason (Verilator cannot parse the `##[0:1]` cycle-delay range) is contradicted by the `hier-icache` row in `upstream_pr_candidates.md`, which records that limitation as gone since 2026-08-07. The replacement reason written at restore time - that the property's cycle delay is a timing construct fatal inside a `--lib-create` block - is ALSO doubtful now: the measured trigger was hyperbus alone, and a property's `##` shows up only as a `STMTDLY` warning. The patch was never validated in isolation. Drop it and rebuild `super_noc`: if the block still verilates, remove the entry and keep only an upstream note.

**Two upstream candidates found on the way**, both recorded in `upstream_pr_candidates.md`: Verilator aborts with an internal error (`V3Broken.cpp`, on `ariane_pkg.sv`) whenever tree dumping is enabled on this design, which is what closed the AST-dump route to the diagnosis; and the refusal message would be far cheaper to act on if it named the offending node's file and line, since it is emitted from a global check that has the node available.

### 5.3.0 A Write/Read Race Inside the Hierarchical Build (2026-08-19)

**The finding.** Under machine load the hierarchical build compiles a child's generated `.cpp` while Verilator is still writing it: gcc reports `expected primary-expression at end of input` partway through a file that is complete and well-formed on disk seconds later. It bit twice - `unterminated #ifndef` in a `V*__pi*.h` on 2026-08-17 (blamed then on cache staleness, which was the wrong diagnosis) and `spatz_cc` inside crux_isle on 2026-08-19, in a build started from an empty `verilator_work/`, which rules staleness out.

**The cause, read off the generated makefile.** A child's verilation rule declares only `<child>.sv` and `<child>.mk` as its outputs while the verilation writes dozens of `.cpp` files, so the moment the `.mk` appears make treats the dependency as satisfied and (under `-j`) starts compiling sources still being written.

**What the flow does about it** (implemented 2026-08-19): `build-sim-verilator` drives the hierarchical build in two explicit phases - it asks the generated makefile for every child `.mk` first, then for `hier_build` - so emission and compilation never overlap. A retry around phase 2 stays as a backstop and announces itself in the log, so a systematic failure is still visible while the race is not. The expected price is the lost pipelining between one child's compilation and another's verilation: cores idle during phase 1, in the order of 5-15% of wall clock on a project with many children, against a class of failure that cost two campaigns in one night. Accepted deliberately.

**Plan B if that price proves too high**: rewrite the child rule's output list in the generated makefile (a `sed` over `V*_hier.mk`) so it declares every emitted file. Each child could then compile as soon as ITS OWN emission completes, restoring the pipelining without reopening the race - at the cost of patching an upstream artifact, which is why the barrier came first.

**Still worth an upstream report**: the incomplete output declaration is the defect; everything above is our containment.

### 5.3.2 Memory Self-Preload: Attempted and Rolled Back (2026-08-18)

**What was tried.** To free the preload memories from the Verilator top unit, each memory isle was given a self-preload block: a clocked counter that, a configurable number of cycles after reset release, executes `$readmemh` into its own banks with the image prefix taken from a runtime plusarg (`+PRELOAD_<instance>=<prefix>`), so the testbench holds no dotted path into the memory. Questa ran it green on all seven examples; mesh ran it green under Verilator too (memories inside tile children). The crossbar family exposed two distinct Verilator 5.050 defects, each costing a debug day and each reproducible:

1. **Protect-lib split of a self-loading hier module.** When the isle ITSELF is the hierarchical block and its top level contains the `$readmemh` with dotted paths into its own subtree, the protect-lib flow silently materializes a SECOND copy of the memory subtree in the parent for those paths to bind to: the readmem writes the shadow (post-write readback correct and persistent), while the SoC datapath reads the DPI child (zeros at every fetch). No warning is emitted. On the NoC family the same code works because the hier module is the TILE and the readmem sits one level below the protected top.
2. **Elimination of readmem-only clocked blocks in the top unit.** With the isle inlined in the top (hier demoted), the four per-bank always blocks exist in the generated C++ - four independent counters, four guarded `VL_READMEM_N` calls with correct targets and filenames, all in one eval function - yet at run time no bank loads; adding a `$display` to an instance makes exactly that instance load (heisenbug). The initial-block form (`wait (rst_ni); #10ns;`) in the same position loads all banks correctly (proven end-to-end: crux Verilator build+run green on 2026-08-16).

**The decision.** Rather than pinning the flow to workarounds stacked on two tool defects, the self-preload mechanism was removed and the testbench-side dotted `$readmemh` restored (the state validated for weeks on both simulators). Preload memories therefore stay in the Verilator top unit BY DESIGN - the measured cost is small (memory isles are arrays, cheap to verilate; the top already carried a shadow copy under defect 1, so demotion is roughly neutral). Revisit only if a Verilator release fixes both defects, or if the nested-boot ladder (section 3.9) makes the plusarg handoff valuable enough to justify the initial-block form plus permanent hier demotion of the memories.

**Upstream reports to file** (with 5.3.1): both defects above. Minimal reproducers: (1) a hier_block module whose top level does a genvar-indexed dotted `$readmemh` into its own generate subtree; (2) the same module inlined, comparing the clocked-counter form against the initial-block form, with and without a `$display` beside the readmem.

### 5.4.1 What the Minimal Set Actually Buys (measured 2026-08-19)

**`bring_up: minimal` is a per-project choice driven by the TEST, not a default to roll out.** The first measurement (noc, 2026-08-17: 11m55s to 9m30s) was read as a general win; a seven-project sweep says otherwise, because the saving comes from blocks the test never touches and the cost comes from blocks it does:

| Project | Boot / bring-up | Run | Same project before | Delta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| noc | jtag / minimal | 9m31s | 9m03s (jtag/minimal) | ~equal |
| noc_isle | jtag / minimal | 11m53s | 9m08s (jtag/all) | **+2m45s** |
| super_crossbar | jtag / minimal | 6m00s | 5m36s (jtag/all) | +24s |
| crux | jtag / minimal | 5m06s | 4m20s (force/all) | +46s |
| noc_subtile | force / all | 13m01s | 8m59s (jtag/all) | **+4m02s** |

Where the offload application exercises every gated block (noc_isle), the firmware ungates them one phase at a time and the phases serialize: the run gets LONGER than a fully-woken SoC. Where gated content stays unused (noc's clusters during boot, super_crossbar's nested macro), it costs nothing to leave it asleep. So the knob's value is "does this test use the gated blocks?", and its second value - exercising the power-down path in a regression, which no fully-woken SoC does - is worth having on at least one project of each family regardless of the clock.

**A second number worth recording**: the same sweep moved noc_subtile from jtag to force and its run went 8m59s -> 13m01s. The architected bring-up is not only cleaner than the forces, it is measurably faster - the force path holds values through a delay and waits on a boot watchdog, the JTAG path writes and proceeds. Keeping one project per family on force stays deliberate (it is a supported boot mode for hosts without a JTAG export), but it is a coverage cost, not a performance choice.

### 5.4 Minimal Bring-Up Set: Gate What the Test Does Not Use

*Implemented on 2026-08-17 and measured on `noc`: `testbench.bring_up: "minimal"` leaves both control groups (16 clusters, 8 L2 tiles) gated at hand-off, the offload firmware enables its target's group when its phase starts and switches it back off when the phase ends. Offload PASS with 16 instances in **9m30s against 11m55s** at full bring-up (-20% on a single-phase test, where the only saving is the boot window; the effect scales with the number of phases). The power-down half of the domain life cycle is now exercised by the regression, which nothing covered before. What remains open is listed at the end of this section.*

Promoted from a 5.2 lever to a specification on 2026-08-17 (user decision), because the force-free bring-up made it implementable cleanly: gating policy is now a MASK in the jtag sequence's SBA writes, not a set of forces.

**Problem.** The bring-up enables every component, and the idle compute clusters then trap-loop at PC 0 — real simulated activity (fetch, trap, jump, every cycle) that both backends pay for: the cycle-driven one enormously, the event-driven one measurably (it was also the source of the 10k-line Illegal Instruction chatter of the first crux run).

**Specification.**

1.  **Static minimal mask (generator + VIP sequence).** The generator derives the boot-critical set — the host, `software_stack.boot_memory`'s group, the UART/EOT path — and unions it with the test application's own targets, which it already auto-discovers (the offload target list). The jtag bring-up writes per-group masks computed from that set instead of all-ones. A `testbench`-level list adds components per scenario; `boot_mode: "force"` projects keep the full-on behaviour (their TB has no per-instance selectivity and stays the legacy-coverage path).
2.  **Dynamic phase gating (firmware).** Everything after the boot handoff belongs to the firmware, not to the generator: the testbench acts once, before the core exists as an agent, and from then on the running program owns the gating. The generator's role here is enabling, not deciding - it emits the register map, the firmware headers and the target-to-group contracts the program manoeuvres with.

    *   **Powering up progressively** (implemented): the firmware enables the group of the target its current phase needs - the offload application already de-isolates and ungates its targets through SYS_CTRL, payload memory included.
    *   **Powering back down** (open, and where the real payoff is): switching a finished target off before moving to the next keeps the simulated cost at roughly one cluster instead of all of them for the whole run - on the supers, the difference between hours and minutes. Powering down is the more delicate direction and needs its own sequence: quiescence first (no transaction in flight), then `axi_isolate` engaged BEFORE clock and reset are removed (otherwise an incoming request hangs on a stopped block and stalls the interconnect), and the FFAR two-phase contract again on the way back up if the target is re-enabled later. Note that this also widens what the test proves - it exercises the power-down/power-up cycle, which is coverage, not only speed - so it should be introduced as a deliberate test feature rather than as a silent optimisation.
3.  **Constraints, all already codified elsewhere**: the FFAR two-phase contract on every enable (clocks, settling window, then reset release); `axi_isolate` engaged before leaving any block gated, so a stray access terminates cleanly instead of hanging the interconnect; the boot memory's group up before the boot handoff.
4.  **To verify on the crossbar family**: that per-isle clock domains are individually gateable at all — the mesh has per-tile gating already.
5.  **Validation and measure**: done on `noc` under QuestaSim (see the note above). **Still open**: the same run under Verilator (where the cycle-driven backend should show a larger saving), the rollout to the other jtag projects, and the measurement on a super with several offload phases in sequence - the case the mechanism exists for. Masks must stay identical between backends so the Questa/Verilator equivalence comparison stays fair.

#### Advantages
*   The largest untouched simulation-time lever after hierarchization, and it costs policy, not infrastructure: both mechanisms (SBA masks, firmware ungating) are green on both simulators today.
*   Kills the idle-cluster trap-loop noise class at the source.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **A test that forgets to enable a component it touches** now hangs or fails instead of silently working.
    *   *Mitigation*: `axi_isolate` makes the failure clean and immediate (error response, not a hang), and the mask derivation unions the declared test targets - forgetting requires touching something the test never declared.
*   **Mask derivation for non-offload apps** (plain hello-world has no target list).
    *   *Mitigation*: hello-world's set is exactly the boot-critical derivation; anything else uses the `testbench` list until a richer app contract exists.

#### The last hierarchical reference of the force path (analysed 2026-08-17, deliberately not acted on)

Under `boot_mode: "force"` one dotted path into the boot memory survives, and it is not the preload: it is the AXI monitor that watches the memory's read port to know when the CPU has started fetching, which is how the testbench decides to release the boot forces. That reference is what keeps the memory inlined in the Verilator top unit under force boot (self-preload removed the other one). Three ways out were mapped:

*   **Move the monitor to the top level.** Possible on the crossbar, but not for free: at the top the memory is fed through an asynchronous CDC FIFO (`async_axi_in_ar_data/_wptr/_rptr`), so the monitor would watch a write-pointer advance and decode the packed AR struct instead of `ar_valid` - tied to the CDC layout rather than to the AXI protocol. **Impossible on the NoC** without new machinery: only floo flits reach the top, and the AXI request materialises inside the tile, after the chimney - and the case is real, since `super_noc` is NoC and force-mode.
*   **Drop the monitor and rely on the time-based release** already configured (`testbench.boot_force_delay_ns`): no hierarchical path at all, at the cost of releasing the forces on a deadline rather than on the observed event.
*   **Do nothing, and let the boot mode decide** - the choice taken. Force mode is the legacy path, kept so the schema default stays under regression; its Verilator dividend is not a goal. Migrating a project to `jtag` removes the monitor and the exclusion together, with no new monitor to maintain.

Revisit only if the force path itself ever needs the hierarchization dividend.

### 5.5 Waveforms Under Verilator: a Capability to Build, Not a Flag to Expose (analysed 2026-08-21)

Batch Verilator runs dump nothing today, and no flag can change that. Tracing is a verilation-time decision (`--trace-fst` changes the generated C++, children included), but the blocker is structural to the hierarchical flow: a boxed block's model object is constructed lazily, by the `*_protectlib_create()` DPI call during the parent's FIRST `eval()`, and only then does it register its trace callbacks in the shared `VerilatedContext`. A trace opened from the testbench's time-zero `$dumpvars` runs the parent's per-instance `initLib()` lookup before any child exists and **silently dumps the parent only**. With the `--main` Verilator generates - our case - it is worse: that main constructs the top with an EMPTY model name, the lookup key gains a leading dot, no child ever matches, their trace codes are never allocated, and the first dump **segfaults inside `fstWriterEmitValueChange`**.

**The cure is owning the main, and a working reference exists**: `MAGIA/target/verilator/src/magia_main.cpp`, run on this very host on 2026-08-20, documents the exact sequence - set `threads()` on the context (it must match `--threads`; unset, the pool sizes to every core of the machine), decide `traceEverOn` BEFORE constructing the model, construct with a non-empty name (`"TOP"`), `eval()` once so the children construct and register, only then `trace()`/`open()`, dump per time slot in the event loop, and `close()` or the FST is truncated. The dump path arrives as a `+FST=<path>` plusarg, so the model is compiled once with support and each run decides whether to trace.

For Ollivander this means: a generated `main.cpp` template replacing `--main`; tracing propagated to the child libraries in the two-phase build; and the thread count wired coherently in what becomes the THIRD place that must agree with `--threads` (after the flag and `-DVL_TIME_CONTEXT` - see the coupling found on 2026-08-21). The real weight is validation, not code: the acceptance criterion must be "signals INSIDE a boxed block appear in the dump", never "the FST file exists", because silent parent-only dumps are precisely this mechanism's failure mode; replacing `--main` touches every Verilator run, waveforms requested or not, so the gate is the full matrix; and MAGIA's own file carries an unexplained warning to reproduce-or-exclude before trusting the non-tracing model - "a non-tracing model diverges on tests that do real memory traffic, for reasons not yet understood".

**Deliberately deferred** (2026-08-21): the practical need is debugging a failed Verilator run, covered today by regenerating under Questa (`make gui`, 45 s build), and none of the four standing priorities passes through it. The one condition that would pull it forward: a defect during the JTAG-load work that manifests ONLY under Verilator - at that point this stops being convenience, and the reference implementation makes the detour short.

## 7. A Linting Flow (Verilator and/or SpyGlass)

The `fast-check` gate verifies the **structural contract**: the generated wrappers elaborate against exact-signature stubs of the external IPs, so connectivity and type mismatches in what Ollivander emits are already caught, license-free, on every project. What no gate covers today is **code quality**: the fast-check runs with Verilator's default warning surface (plus three suppressions), and the hand-written SystemVerilog in `components/` is only ever exercised through whichever generate branches the seven example configurations happen to elaborate. The class of defect this leaves invisible is exactly what the Verilator simulation campaign kept tripping on in third-party code — mixed blocking/non-blocking drives to one struct, latches, incomplete cases, width mismatches — and there is no reason to believe our own components are immune; a lint gate would report those at negligible cost instead of at simulation-build time.

Two tiers are worth evaluating, not mutually exclusive:

*   **Verilator lint** (license-free): a `lint` target reusing the fast-check infrastructure with a curated warning set (`WIDTH`, `LATCH`, `UNUSED`, `UNDRIVEN`, `CASEINCOMPLETE`, `BLKANDNBLK`, ...), scoped to `components/` and to the generated output, with per-component tops so that generate branches not elaborated by any example still get checked. Seconds of runtime, CI-friendly, and aligned in spirit with the lowRISC style guide the components already follow.
*   **SpyGlass** (licensed, deeper): the astral precedent — note that astral's lint is a **SpyGlass** flow, not a Verilator one; `bender script verilator` appears there only as the flist *format* fed to SpyGlass. A structural lint of that depth (CDC/RDC candidates, richer rule decks) would be a second-stage gate; Synopsys tooling exists under `/tools/synopsys`, license availability to be assessed.

#### Advantages
*   **Catches the simulation campaign's defect classes in our own code before they bite**: every issue found in third-party IPs during the Verilator bring-up (mixed-style struct drives above all) is a defect a lint would have flagged for free had it lived in `components/`.
*   **Coverage beyond the example configurations**: per-component linting elaborates branches no example instantiates, which is the standing blind spot of both `fast-check` and the simulation suite.
*   **Nearly free on the Verilator tier**: the flist generation, stubs and tool invocation all exist; what is new is a warning-set policy and a make target.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Warning-set policy is the real work**: too strict and the gate cries wolf on inherited idioms; too lax and it catches nothing. The suppression comments in `sim.mk.mako` show every choice must be argued.
    *   *Mitigation*: start from the messages the simulation campaign actually hit (they have proven cost), enable incrementally, and document each suppression inline as the vsim flow already does.
*   **Third-party noise**: linting anything that pulls in external IPs drowns the signal.
    *   *Mitigation*: scope to `components/` and generated output only; the IPs have their own upstreams (and our patch/PR pipeline for what we find).
*   **SpyGlass adds license coupling to a flow whose sibling exists precisely to avoid it.**
    *   *Mitigation*: keep the tiers independent — Verilator lint as the always-on CI gate, SpyGlass as an on-demand deep pass, mirroring the QuestaSim/Verilator split of the simulation flow.

### 7.1 The Self-Elaboration Check Is Wired; the Linting Tiers Above Are Not

Delivered and no longer open: phase 11 elaborates the generated RTL with the `slang` front-end on every generation run, `fast-check` repeats it over its stubbed file list, and the policy is `generated_rtl_check` (see `env_configuration_guide.md` chapter 6). Both passes are green on all nine examples.

What that does NOT do is the code-quality work this chapter is about. The check is a front-end elaboration: it finds what is ill-formed, not what is ill-advised. Latches, incomplete cases, width truncations and mixed blocking/non-blocking drives all elaborate perfectly and are exactly what the two tiers above would report. So the chapter stands as written; the self-elaboration is a floor, not a substitute.

## 8. Multi-Simulator Compatibility (Xcelium, VCS)

Declaring Ollivander compatible with every major simulator would be a substantial selling point, and after the 2026-08-07 license outage it is also risk mitigation: the three vendors run three independent FlexLM servers on this site, so a lapse in one leaves the others standing. Surveyed on 2026-08-08, while QuestaSim was down: **Xcelium 25.03 works today** (license checked out, a probe design compiled, elaborated and simulated), the Cadence features all run to 31-dec-2026, and the bulk of the Synopsys features do too. **VCS 2024.09 is installed completely but unusable as provisioned**: its module resolves the legacy 32-bit `linux` target (the installation only carries `linux64`, compiler present), and with the architecture overridden the platform check rejects the host kernel string — both environment matters to resolve outside this repository. Priority follows availability: Xcelium first, VCS when its environment is fixed.

The architecture is already shaped for this: `SIM_TOOLS` / `FAST_CHECK_TOOLS` are lists by design, the suite's pass criterion (UART output + EOT in the transcript) is tool-agnostic, and Bender emits `vcs` scripts natively (for Xcelium, verify `bender script` support at the pinned version or reuse the generic-flist route the Verilator flow validated).

#### Advantages
*   **License-risk decorrelation**: a regression that can attest on any one of three commercial engines plus Verilator never stalls on a single vendor's renewal.
*   **Defect yield**: every engine has found defect classes the others tolerate (Verilator alone: the silent icache define, the pragma-comment trap, the 2-state exposure of the never-parked snitch cores). A third and fourth engine extend that coverage.
*   **Marketability**: "generates environments for Questa, Xcelium, VCS and Verilator" is a concrete differentiator for the tool.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **The cost is the probe campaign, not the scripts**: the Verilator enablement measured it - compile legs came cheap, the iterative probing of latent RTL and flag issues was the real effort.
    *   *Mitigation*: replay the Verilator playbook (one SoC per topology family first, message-policy tables per tool, findings routed to the registry/upstream pipeline), and expect per-tool fossils.
*   **Suite semantics must stay unified**: per-tool summary lines, `check-tested` attestation and log layout already handle two engines; a third must slot into the same scheme, not fork it.
    *   *Mitigation*: the `SIM_TOOLS` loop in `ollivander_test.mk` is the single extension point; add engines there only.

## 9. A Trace/Performance Measurement Pipeline

The offload tests make the examples COMPUTE for the first time, and the next natural question after "does it pass" is "how fast" - none of Ollivander's flows measures anything yet. Gwaihir ships the only complete measurement stack seen in the references (2026-08-09 analysis): the cores' unconditional `.dasm`/`.log` traces are turned into per-hart performance JSONs (`gen_trace.py`), source-annotated listings (`annotate.py` + addr2line, host and cluster alike), merged (`join.py`), cut into labelled regions of interest by Mako ROI specs (`roi.py`), and rendered as Chrome-trace timelines (`visualize.py`); an experiment framework fits analytical models on top. All of it operates on trace formats our instantiated IPs already emit (snitch harts, CVA6) - the pipeline is IP-side, not testbench-side, so most of it could be adopted rather than rebuilt.

*   **Advantages**: turns the offload family from smoke tests into benchmarks; gives the generated SoCs a per-hart timeline for debugging cross-domain interactions (which the noc_isle-class findings would have benefited from); the ROI-spec idea maps naturally onto the generated firmware's phases (the generator knows where the phases are and could emit the ROI spec itself).
*   **Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies**: the upstream scripts are snitch-repo-shaped (paths, hart numbering conventions) - start by driving them as-is on a mesh project's logs before considering any integration; trace volume is significant - keep it an on-demand target (`make traces`-style), never part of the suite; the host-side story differs per host IP - begin with the snitch harts only. Schedule after the VIP (chapter 2.1): the two share the "measure through what the IPs already provide" philosophy but neither depends on the other.

## 10. The Test Suite's Progress Report (and the Step It Buys Toward Parallel Runs)

`soc_cfg_examples/test_summary.log` is written by `log_step`, which appends one line per completed step from **sixteen scattered call sites** in `ollivander_test.mk`. The file is therefore history and nothing else: a running campaign cannot be inspected from it, and an interrupted one leaves no trace of the steps that never ran, so "where is it now" needs `ps` and log spelunking instead of a file read.

**The proposal (2026-08-20).** Write the file at the START of the run, with the header valued as it is today plus **every expected entry of every selected project**, each carrying a placeholder instead of an outcome. Mark an entry `Started at <time>...` when its step begins, and replace it with the outcome and duration when the step ends - the format the file already has. The report becomes a live table: what is done, what is running and since when, what is still pending. An aborted campaign then shows exactly which step was in flight, which is precisely what is hardest to reconstruct today.

#### Advantages
*   **Progress is a file read.** During the campaigns of 2026-08-20 every "how is it going?" needed process inspection; this answers it, including how long the current step has been running.
*   **Failure localization comes free**: a killed or hung run keeps its `Started at` marker, so the step that never returned is visible without touching a log.
*   **It is the representation parallel runs require** (section 5.2.0). With projects in sequence an append-only log is adequate because write order equals execution order; with projects running concurrently the appends interleave and the file stops being readable. A table indexed by project and step, updated in place, does not have that problem.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **The expected-step list would be computed twice and could diverge from what actually runs** - `TEST_SIM=0`, a per-project `FAST_CHECK_TOOLS_<p>` / `SIM_TOOLS_<p>` override, a missing project directory. A report promising entries nobody will populate is worse than no placeholders at all.
    *   *Mitigation*: compute the list once and consume it twice, for pre-population and for execution. That means factoring today's sixteen `log_step` call sites into one place, which is a maintenance win on its own: sixteen scattered sites are how a step silently stops being reported.
*   **In-place update against an append-only writer.** Patching a line by label is ambiguous, since every project block repeats the same labels.
    *   *Mitigation*: regenerate the whole file from the run's state on each transition - it is tens of lines - rather than editing lines in place.
*   **`make check-tested` reads this file** and accepts a run whose summary ends with `Final Result : SUCCESS`. A pre-populated file must be truncated at the start of the run and must NOT carry a `Final Result` line until the end, or an aborted campaign could be read as a green one.
    *   *Mitigation*: write `Final Result` only in the closing step, and treat its absence as the signal that the run did not complete - which is also what makes the aborted-run case above safe.
*   **The single-writer assumption** that 5.2.0 lists as a blocker for parallel runs is strengthened, not removed, by whole-file rewrites.
    *   *Mitigation*: give each project its own small state file and let a renderer compose the summary from all of them. That removes the shared writer entirely, and is the reason this task is worth doing before the parallel-run one rather than after.

## 11. Continuous Integration: for Ollivander Itself, and for the SoCs It Generates

The CI question is double, and the two halves share machinery but not an owner. **CI for Ollivander** validates the generator on every change - it is the automated form of the `check-tested` ritual, and its job bodies already exist as `make test-all` subsets. **CI for the generated SoC** is a product feature: a user who generates a project gets simulation Makefiles today, and could get a working pipeline around them. Ollivander is expected to be a predominantly (possibly exclusively) CHIPS-IT-internal tool (stated 2026-08-21), so the emitted CI comes in three levels (decided the same day): the **free pipeline as the default** (no assumptions about runners or licenses, works anywhere), the **CHIPS-IT non-free profile one switch away** (Questa legs, pinned modules, mirroring Ollivander's own pipeline), and **custom non-free profiles** as a supported mechanism rather than a template edit. References analysed on 2026-08-21: PR #3 (`yt/dry-run`, the draft to build on), astral and MAGIA (CHIPS-IT projects, the infrastructural references), gwaihir (ETH collaboration, a quarry of technical patterns), and astral's internal `nonfree` `ci.yml` (read with repository access).

### 11.1 The Reference Architecture

All three references share one shape: **two infrastructures and a bridge**. On GitHub (public runners, no licenses): `lint.yml` (license-header check via `pulp-actions/lint-license`, Verible via `verible-linter-action`) plus `gitlab-ci.yml`, which executes nothing - the `pulp-platform/pulp-actions/gitlab-ci` action triggers the pipeline of a **mirror repository** on `gitlab.chips.it` and polls it to completion (`poll-count: 2160`), so the licensed pipeline's verdict lands on the GitHub PR as a status. Internally, astral and MAGIA add a second indirection: the visible 27-line pipeline clones a private `nonfree` repository **at a pinned commit** and spawns a child pipeline from the `ci.yml` inside it, keeping tech- and license-adjacent content out of the public repo (astral's nonfree lives on `gitlab.chips.it`, MAGIA's on ETH's `iis-git` - the pattern survives both organizations); gwaihir instead keeps its 151-line pipeline in-repo with `include:`d templates.

Patterns worth copying, from astral's internal `ci.yml` and gwaihir's pipeline: tool versions pinned as pipeline variables (`questa/2025.3`, `bender/0.28.2` - upgrades become reviewable diffs); two artifact tiers, both `when: always` (6-hour build state handed to downstream jobs, 1-week transcripts and reports); the *"To re-run this test in your shell call: make ..."* echo before every run, so a CI failure carries its own local reproduction command; `rules:` distinguishing scheduled (nightly) from `manual + allow_failure` jobs inside one pipeline; runner `tags:` and per-job `timeout:` everywhere; a Synopsys **elaboration-only synthesis gate** grepping reports for inferred latches, timing loops and unresolved references (a tier we do not even have in chapter 7 yet); gwaihir's transcript greps (`SUCCESS`, expected UART string, `Fatal:`, and the expected-nonzero-exit handling our chapter 3 notes as missing) plus its uv/bender caches. One negative lesson, equally instructive: half of astral's internal file is commented out (FPGA, Linux, board farm) - ambition entered CI faster than it could be kept green. Start narrow and green.

PR #3 (`feat(CI): add draft CI`, 2026-07-03) is the right skeleton in the house style - reusable workflow (`ci.yml` calling `build.yml` via `workflow_call`), `concurrency` with cancel-in-progress, Python+uv+`make setup`, `pulp-actions/bender-install` - with known gaps: the push trigger names `master` while the repository publishes `main` (dead on push, alive on PRs), it generates a single project (`crossbar`) and stops there, and it predates `test-all`, the Verilator flow and fast-check maturity, i.e. everything that would give it content. It is the branch to revive, not to discard.

### 11.2 CI for Ollivander Itself

**Precondition, confirmed planned on 2026-08-21: a mirror `githubmirrors/ollivander` on `gitlab.chips.it`.** The bridge cannot exist without it; nothing else below depends on infrastructure we do not already have.

The tier structure writes itself from the measured budgets (all 2026-08-20/21, warm cache): generation of all seven projects ~8 min, fast-check both tools all seven ~12 min, full dual-tool `test-all` with simulations 4h19.

| Tier | Where | When | Content | Budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | GitHub public runner | push + PR events | lint (license headers + Verible); generation of all seven; fast-check **verilator** of all seven | ~15 min |
| 1 | internal mirror, via bridge | push + PR events | fast-check **questa** of all seven; one full simulation per family (`noc`, `crossbar`, both tools) | < 1h30 |
| 2 | internal mirror, `schedule:` | nightly | full `make test-all`, seven projects, both tools; later the synth-elab gate of chapter 7 | ~4-5h |

The trigger set is deliberately `push` on the active branches PLUS `pull_request`, not PR-only: today's practice pushes to a personal branch with no PRs at all, so a PR-only CI would never fire - the same defect as the draft's dead `master` filter. The push leg gives value under the current workflow immediately; the PR leg becomes the integration gate when the OneFlow MR practice starts for real. Which branches trigger which tier is a phase-1 detail (astral fires both tiers on plain `[push, pull_request]`).

Implementation phases, each independently shippable:

1. **Revive PR #3 (no infrastructure needed).** Fix the trigger to `main`, turn the single generation into the seven-project set, add the Verilator fast-check leg and a `lint.yml` alla astral. One decision inside: apt's Verilator on `ubuntu-22.04` is a 4.x, useless for the hierarchical+timing flow - and a merely-recent one is not enough either, because the flow leans on version-sensitive internals (`--hierarchical-params-file` is "internal use"; the `VL_TIME_CONTEXT` coupling was measured on 5.050), so tier 0 must run THE SAME version as the internal module or its verdict validates something else. The verified route (Docker Hub checked 2026-08-21): the Verilator project publishes official images per version, `verilator/verilator:v5.050` included - an organization image built `FROM` it plus Python and bender, published once on ghcr.io and bumped together with the internal module, gives zero per-run install time and an exact reviewable pin. Fallback without Docker: build the `v5.050` tag from source in the workflow behind `actions/cache` (~15 min cold on a 2-core runner, seconds warm). oss-cad-suite was considered and rejected for this job: it ships whatever its nightly bundles, which is precisely the version drift the parity argument forbids.
2. **The bridge (needs the mirror).** Astral's workflow verbatim: `pulp-actions/gitlab-ci` with `domain: gitlab.chips.it`, the mirror repo path, a `GITLAB_TOKEN` secret, and the repository-name guard in the `if:`. Internal pipeline shape: **the hybrid gwaihir actually runs** (settled 2026-08-21, once Ollivander's steady-state scope - synthesis flow and technology - was stated). Gwaihir keeps its simulation pipeline in-repo and triggers its physical-design part as a CHILD pipeline whose `ci.yml` arrives as an artifact from a restricted repository fetched at init - indirection for exactly the content that needs it. For Ollivander: generation and simulation jobs live in-repo (module names and make targets are not secrets, and the repo is internal anyway); the synthesis/technology jobs, when they arrive, come as a nonfree child subpipe, because their pipeline text will reference NDA material - the same boundary the repository already draws at file level by keeping `target/asic/ihp13/pdk` untracked. Consequence for phasing: no nonfree repository is a prerequisite of phase 2, but creating one becomes a prerequisite of the synthesis phase. Yvan's PR #3 is silent on all of this (GitHub tier only; its `secrets: inherit` is the lone hint of future bridge plumbing).
3. **The nightly and the artifacts.** One shape to adopt when the firmware grows beyond today's two apps: astral's internal pipeline runs its offload tests as a `parallel: matrix:` over test names (its `pulpd_offloader_blocking` flow is the same host-embedded-payload architecture Ollivander already generates - confirmed 2026-08-21 against Yvan's notes on `make car-all`), so the nightly tier can matrix over `TEST_APP` once there is a suite to matrix over. `rules: schedule` for tier 2; artifacts = `soc_cfg_examples/test_summary.log` (the pass/fail table `check-tested` already reads) plus per-project transcripts, `when: always`; the chapter-10 report becomes the natural artifact once it exists. The job bodies stay `make test-all TEST_PROJECTS=... SIM_TOOLS=...` invocations - the suite already owns the UART+EOT criterion, the per-step logs and the summary, so CI adds scheduling and reporting, not test logic.

#### Advantages
*   **The suite is already the CI body**: no new test logic, no drift between "what CI checks" and "what a developer runs" - the same `test-all` line, and `check-tested` becomes automated instead of ritual.
*   **The license boundary is already drawn**: the Verilator flow exists precisely so that meaningful validation runs without Siemens licenses, which is what a public runner is.
*   **Each phase pays alone**: tier 0 catches generation and elaboration regressions on every PR even if the mirror never materializes.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Verilator version parity on public runners**: solved by the pinned organization image above; the residual duty is bumping image and internal module together, one reviewable diff.
*   **Shared internal runners and Questa license count**: runner tags for the heavy jobs, nightly scheduled off-hours, and the deliberately-unplanned project parallelization (5.2.0) becomes relevant here - its blockers (peak RAM, license count, single-writer summary) are exactly a CI scheduler's parameters.
*   **Keeping it green**: start with the table above and nothing else; astral's commented-out half is what growing faster than the maintenance budget looks like.

### 11.3 A Generated CI for User SoC Projects

Ollivander already generates the simulation Makefiles; the same knowledge can emit a pipeline around them. The pass criterion (UART + EOT), the target names, the tool tiers and even the user's tuned flags (the `simulation:` section, guide chapter 6) are all in the generated tree - a CI file wrapping them is scaffolding, not invention.

**The one genuinely new design decision: this is SCAFFOLDING, not a regenerated artifact.** A `.gitlab-ci.yml` must live committed at the user's repository root, while everything Ollivander generates is git-ignored by design. So the shape is a one-time emission the user then owns (a `make ci-init` target, or a generator flag, writing a starter pipeline and never touching it again) - a category Ollivander does not have today, and the reason this section is a design task rather than a template-writing task.

**Three levels, one skeleton (decided 2026-08-21).** Every emitted pipeline shares the stage shape (`generate -> fast-check -> build-sim -> run-sim` against the project's own Makefile), the pass criterion (UART + EOT), and the two artifact tiers (build state short-lived, transcripts and summary a week, `when: always`); the levels differ only in the tool legs and the site assumptions:

*   **Default: the FREE pipeline.** `SIM_TOOLS=verilator`, no module pins, no runner tags - it assumes nothing about where it runs, which is what a default must do, and it is the pipeline that works even outside CHIPS-IT.
*   **One switch away: the CHIPS-IT non-free profile** (e.g. `make ci-init CI_PROFILE=chips-it`). Adds the Questa legs, pins the tool modules as pipeline variables alla astral - legitimate because the target runners are known - and the runner tags for heavy jobs. Its shape mirrors Ollivander's own internal pipeline deliberately: whoever maintains one can read the other, and improvements migrate by diff.
*   **Custom non-free profiles as a mechanism, not a template edit.** The site-specific content of a profile (module names and versions, runner tags, extra tool legs) is exactly the kind of thing the ENVIRONMENT layer already owns - `ollivander_config.yml` and the `-a` overlay - so a profile is a declared block there, the shipped `chips-it` profile is one instance of it, and a third-party site writes its own without touching Ollivander. The SoC description stays site-agnostic, as it must: a YAML that generates the same SoC everywhere cannot be the place where a runner tag lives.

#### Advantages
*   **A generated SoC arrives CI-ready**: the gap between "it simulates on the designer's machine" and "it is regression-tested" closes at generation time.
*   **The user's `simulation:` knobs flow in for free**, because the pipeline calls the Makefile the knobs are already cooked into - no second place to configure.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Scaffold drift**: the user edits their copy, Ollivander improves the template, the two diverge silently. Mitigation: a generator-version comment in the emitted file, and documentation stating plainly that regeneration never updates it.
*   **The chips-it profile is a second thing to keep current** (module versions drift, runner tags change): one declared block in the default environment config, versioned with the generator, is the single place - and its diffs are reviewable the way astral's pinned variables are.
*   **Tool provisioning is two different problems now**: on CHIPS-IT runners the pinned module variables ARE the provisioning (astral's pattern, safe because the infrastructure is known); the Verilator-only variant still pins nothing machine-specific and documents what it needs, since its runners are by definition not ours.
*   **Dialect choice is settled by the internal-first premise**: GitLab CI is the deliverable; a GitHub Actions twin only if generated projects ever get public mirrors with the bridge, and not before.

## 12. Type-Aware Tie-Offs

Closed and moved out of this document: the chip wrapper's two broken directions and the 25 errors this chapter used to list. The behaviour is in `padframe_configuration_guide.md` section 2.1 and `env_configuration_guide.md` chapter 6; the residue was not a Padrick style issue after all but our own wrapper instantiating the padframe without passing its register-interface types, so the whole chain elaborated as `logic`.

What the last of those findings left behind is a real question. Ollivander ties unconnected inputs to `'0` regardless of their type, and `apb_subsystem_isle` drove `'0` onto an `lc_tx_t` - OpenTitan's life-cycle type, redundantly encoded, where `Off` is `4'b1010` and all-zeros is neither `On` nor `Off`. It worked because a conforming receiver reads an invalid value as "not On": safe by luck, not by construction. That instance is fixed in its own template, which was the right place for it.

The general case is not fixed. A tie-off to zero on any enum drives whatever state happens to be encoded as zero, and if that state is legitimate and wrong, nothing reports it - slang only objected here because the encoding is redundant enough to make `'0` invalid outright. A type-aware tie-off would consult the type: an enum's designated safe value where one exists, `'0` only for plain vectors. The information is available (the isle headers are already parsed with pyslang), so this is a matter of deciding the convention - how a component declares which value is safe - rather than of finding the data.

## 13. MAGIA as an Ollivander Consumer: the Tile as a Macro, the Top as a Project

**Direction set by the user, 2026-08-30.** MAGIA is a large, multi-person, still-evolving project; being able to propose Ollivander as the generator of BOTH its tile (exported as a macro) and its top level would be a major value-add for CHIPS-IT. The goal is an equivalent project, never a bit-identical one - and never an architectural makeover either: what MAGIA chose deliberately is a requirement to honor, not a gap to "upgrade" away. The explicit first instance of that rule: MAGIA is HOSTLESS by design (the testbench preloads the images and drives the entry points; there is no host SoC), so this line of work requires Ollivander to learn the host-optional SoC class rather than proposing a manager tile.

**What tonight's reconnaissance already established** (2026-08-30, read-only, on the local checkout): `hw/mesh/magia.sv` plus hand-written `noc_configs/` on FlooNoC 0.6.1 instantiated directly (no floogen) - structurally the exact shape our NoC family generates, including the collectives this wave delivers (with the software injection path MAGIA drives by hand); `hw/tile/` is the real content: a CV32 core with CV-X-IF, RedMulE, iDMA (OBI/AXI converters), an L1 SPM, an event unit, and `fractal_sync` - DMA, RedMulE and synchronization are driven by CUSTOM CORE INSTRUCTIONS through XIF decoders, and the sync tree is a physical network SEPARATE from the NoC. Most tile IPs are already in our catalogue (redmule, idma, cv32e40p), and our forced axi fork IS the MAGIA-lineage multicast fork.

**Known gaps for the deep-analysis session to size** (the list to be completed, not the final one):
*   **Hostless SoCs**: the schema requires a host today; boot/preload/EOT machinery assumes one. MAGIA's model is testbench-driven preload - ironically our force/readmemh lineage - so the class exists in embryo; what is missing is making "no host" a first-class, validated configuration (who owns the boot contract, what the EOT criterion becomes, what the VIP drives).
*   **The tile as an Ollivander project exported as a macro**: the two-level generation the supers already prove (per-coordinate identity as a port, byte-stable naming) applied to a tile whose content is core+accelerators rather than a full SoC. To size: what of `hw/tile/` becomes components/isles, what stays a wrapped IP.
*   **A second, non-NoC network** (`fractal_sync`): inter-tile wiring outside FlooNoC. Ollivander has no concept for it; the analysis must decide between a generic "secondary network" schema notion and treating it as exported tile pins the top wires by pattern.
*   **An instruction-driven control model** (XIF): our Offload contract kinds are host-driven memory-mapped; MAGIA's cores control everything from the instruction stream. Likely a third contract kind (or the recognition that a hostless SoC needs no offload contract at all - the payload IS the firmware).
*   **FlooNoC version distance**: their 0.6.1 tile-side interfaces against our 0.8.4+ emission; the tile wrap has to absorb it, or their bump does.

**Shape**: one dedicated deep-analysis session on the MAGIA tree (hw, flow, testbench, configs), producing an inventory (hand-written vs generatable), the completed gap list with effort estimates, and the wave plan. Scheduled by the user after the collectives wave; the deliverable of the session is the roadmap, not code.

#### Advantages
*   **A second real consumer family for Ollivander** beyond the astral/gwaihir lineage, with many active developers downstream of the generated fleet, regression and collectives machinery.
*   **Convergence with the MAGIA team's collective work**: this wave's stamper/injection path is exactly what their hand-driven RTL flow lacks.

#### Difficulties & Mitigation Strategies
*   **Respecting deliberate architecture while generalizing the generator**: every gap above must be closed as an Ollivander FEATURE (host-optional, secondary networks, new contract kind), never as a MAGIA-side concession.
    *   *Mitigation*: the analysis session states, per gap, which side owns the change and why; anything that would bend MAGIA's architecture is reported as out of scope.
*   **A moving target**: MAGIA evolves while we analyze.
    *   *Mitigation*: anchor the analysis to one named revision of their tree, and record it.
